# Wall-clock cost of PSBD against a plain forward pass

## Question

The deployed placement runs PSBD at k = 3 forward passes per sample, the PSBD
paper's own value. A reader of the paper needs to know what that costs against
a single unperturbed pass, and whether raising k to 5, 10 or 20 buys anything
worth the extra wall clock, on the hardware this project actually runs on.

## Method

`measure.py` loads a backdoored ViT-B/16 checkpoint
(`vit_cifar100_badnet_a2o_0_1`) and a backdoored Swin-S checkpoint
(`swin_cifar100_badnet_a2o_0_1`) through `models.backbones.load_checkpoint`,
and draws 512 clean CIFAR-100 test images from the standardized PSBD
validation split (`data.splits.build_psbd_loaders_from_checkpoint`). The
images are moved to the GPU once, at their native 32x32 resolution, and reused
unperturbed for every timed configuration, so no data-loading time enters any
measurement. The model's own `Sequential(Resize(224), network)` wrapper does
the upscale internally, exactly as every other PSBD forward pass does.

Every configuration times batch 128, fp32, no autocast, `torch.no_grad()`, 1
warm-up iteration discarded and 3 repeats timed with
`torch.cuda.synchronize()` bracketing each one. 3 configurations are timed. A
plain forward pass with no perturbation attached. PSBD with `token_mask` at
`before_attention_norm`, the recommended placement, plugged through
`models.positions.plug_dropout` at rate 0.3, for k in 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20
forward passes. The same sweep with `dropout` at `post_residual`, the PSBD
paper's own ConvNet placement, timed to check whether the cost is set by k
alone or also by which position and operator is probed.

    PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python experiments/psbd_cost/measure.py

Writes `results/_experiments/psbd_cost/cost.json`. Needs a GPU, ran here on
the login-node NVIDIA A100-PCIE-40GB, torch 2.6.0+cu124, and finished in under
7 minutes.

## Finding

Cost is set by k, not by which position or operator carries the probe. At
k = 3 the token_mask placement and the dropout placement agree to within 4% on
ViT-B/16 (3.04x against 3.18x the plain pass) and within 1% on Swin-S (3.03x
against 3.07x), and the same holds at every other k. The 2 operators here do
O(1) work next to a whole transformer block, so this is what the measurement
predicts rather than a coincidence.

Seconds per input for the recommended placement, token_mask at
before_attention_norm, against a plain unperturbed pass:

| k | ViT-B/16 s/input | ViT-B/16 slowdown | Swin-S s/input | Swin-S slowdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain | 0.0022 | 1.00x | 0.0017 | 1.00x |
| 1 | 0.0022 | 1.01x | 0.0017 | 1.01x |
| 3 | 0.0067 | 3.04x | 0.0051 | 3.03x |
| 5 | 0.0112 | 5.10x | 0.0085 | 5.05x |
| 10 | 0.0226 | 10.31x | 0.0169 | 10.10x |
| 20 | 0.0460 | 20.94x | 0.0339 | 20.22x |

The slowdown scales almost exactly linearly in k on both architectures, since
a k-pass PSBD sweep is k independent forward calls over the same batch with no
shared work between them, and the 1 warm-up pass and probe-plugging overhead
are a fixed cost amortized over 512 images. The paper's own k = 3 default
costs about 3 times a plain pass, and the k = 20 point the Monte Carlo pass
count experiment finds useful at 1% poisoning
(`experiments/monte_carlo_passes/`) costs about 21 times a plain pass. Swin-S
is consistently faster per input than ViT-B/16 at every k, by about 25% to
30%, plain pass included, so the relative slowdown from raising k is what
transfers across architectures, not the absolute per-input cost. The table is
`paper/tables/cost.tex` and its macros are `paper/tables/cost.macros.json`,
both generated by `scripts/paper/tab_cost.py` from this experiment's
`cost.json`.
