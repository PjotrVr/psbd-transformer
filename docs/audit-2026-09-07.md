## A2, the batch coupled Gaussian

`GaussianNoise.forward` computed `scale = x.detach().std()`, reduced over batch,
tokens and channels together, so a sample's perturbation magnitude depended on
which other samples shared its batch. Every other operator in the file draws per
sample. The validation, clean and backdoor splits hold different image
populations, so the 3 sat at 3 different noise levels while every comparison
between them assumes 1.

Measured before fixing, activation std per split relative to the clean split:

| checkpoint | position | backdoor vs clean | validation vs clean |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `vit_cifar10_badnet_a2o_0_1` | `before_mlp` | -0.75% | -0.03% |
| `vit_cifar10_badnet_a2o_0_1` | `before_attention_norm` | +13.16% | +0.20% |
| `vit_cifar100_badnet_a2o_0_1` | `before_mlp` | -1.10% | -0.05% |
| `vit_cifar100_badnet_a2o_0_1` | `before_attention_norm` | +32.30% | -0.01% |
| `vit_cifar100_blend_0_1` | `before_mlp` | +2.27% | -0.06% |
| `vit_cifar100_blend_0_1` | `before_attention_norm` | +15.43% | +0.07% |

The validation split is clean everywhere, so the threshold was always calibrated
at the right noise level. `before_mlp`, which carries the rank 2 configuration and
the H23 mechanism claim, sits inside 2.3% and is safe. `before_attention_norm` is
not: the backdoor split was perturbed 13 to 32% harder than the clean split it is
scored against, which raises backdoor PSU and drags AUROC down.

The obvious hypothesis was that this explained the published failure.
`docs/results/negative-results.md` section 7 reports gaussian at
`before_attention_norm` on CIFAR-100 badnet 1% at AUROC 0.168, and the worst
coupling above is CIFAR-100 badnet at +32.3%.

**The re-sweep refutes that hypothesis.** On 50 rebuilt badnet cells the mean
AUROC delta is +0.004 with a range of -0.043 to +0.067, and the count of inverted
cells is 37 before and 37 after. The headline cell moves 0.168 to 0.191. Section
7's explanation stands on its own merits.

The reason a 13 to 32 percent noise difference moves AUROC so little is that AUROC
is a ranking statistic: raising the noise on every backdoor sample shifts that
split together, which moves a fixed threshold far more than it moves the ordering.
The threshold-dependent numbers still need re-checking.

The fix remains correct. A sample's perturbation must not depend on its batch
neighbours, and every other operator already drew per sample. It simply does not
rescue this particular result, and recording that is the point of having run the
comparison rather than assuming it.

The 4649 superseded caches are preserved under `archive/gaussian_batchstd/`
rather than deleted, so the before and after comparison stays possible.

They were moved OUT of `results/<folder>/psbd/` rather than renamed in place, and
that detail is load bearing. Every analysis script discovers position configs by
listing that directory, and a folder named `<position>_gaussian_batchstd` parses
as operator `dropout` at a position that does not exist, which is exactly the
mislabelling recorded as A4. Renaming in place would have re-admitted the buggy
measurement to the ranking under a phantom name. The archive keeps each folder's
original name so a comparison script reads it the same way it read the live tree.

Only 1049 of the 4649 are being re-measured. The remainder are SAM checkpoints,
which are out of scope, or attacks that never implanted, where there is no
backdoor to detect. Nothing reads the archived copies.

## A5, oracle versus deployable TPR

`defence_tables.py` computed TPR by reading the ROC curve of the analysis pool,
which places the threshold to hit the target FPR on the very clean and poisoned
split the detector exists to find. `psbd_operating_points.py` already called that
`oracle_fpr` and marked it not deployable, but the report labelled the columns
plainly as `TPR@1%` and `TPR@5%`.

Both are now reported. AUROC never depended on the threshold and is unchanged.
The deployable threshold is the quantile of clean validation score, exactly the
paper's own rule.

The substantive result is that the validation quantile does not transfer: of 192
operating points, 126 overspend their target FPR budget on the analysis pool. The
gap in the intended direction can be large, for example CIFAR-10 blend at 10%
poisoning at a 1% target, deployable 0.327 against oracle 0.722.

## A7, the double Bonferroni

`multi_probe_detection` set its threshold to `quantile(val_min_rank, target/k)`.
The combined score is already a minimum over k probes, so its own `target/k`
quantile is a far rarer event than any single probe reaching `target/k`, and the
achieved FPR came out near `target/k`.

Verified on synthetic uniform probes at k = 4:

| target FPR | old achieved FPR | old TPR | corrected FPR | corrected TPR |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.01 | 0.0008 | 0.027 | 0.0095 | 0.134 |
| 0.05 | 0.0132 | 0.165 | 0.0542 | 0.379 |
| 0.10 | 0.0255 | 0.241 | 0.0970 | 0.541 |
| 0.25 | 0.0648 | 0.428 | 0.2550 | 0.774 |

H41's recorded FPR of about 0.068 at a nominal 0.25 is the signature of this. TPR
was understated throughout. AUROC is unaffected, so the 0.951 headline stands.

The number is arithmetically reproducible, bit for bit, from the raw tensors. It
is still not defensible, and the reason is the confound this project names as its
own founding mistake and used to refute H1.

**The protocol that produces it**, recovered rather than documented in one place:

`gain_scale` at `mlp_norm_out` against `dropout` at `pre_residual`.

**The 2 arms are read at different disturbances.**

| arm | rate | clean validation sigma |
|---|---:|---|

| `gain_scale` at `mlp_norm_out` | 2.0 | 0.953 to 0.977, mean **0.964** |
| `token_mask` at `before_attention_norm` | 0.4 | 0.692 to 0.763, mean 0.713 |

The sigma gap is **+0.255**, the same size as the reported effect. The cause is the
rate grid: gain_scale's sigma jumps 0.395 to 0.968 between rate 1 and rate 2, so
nothing lands near 0.6. `defence_tables.py`'s own tolerance check flags **40 of 48**

**At matched shift ratio, over the full 48-cell panel, it does not win.**

| target sigma | token_mask minus dropout | gain_scale minus dropout | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.2 | +0.093 | **-0.122** | 46 |
| 0.4 | +0.103 | **-0.052** | 48 |
| 0.6 | +0.089 | **-0.007** | 48 |
| 0.8 | +0.081 | **+0.048** | 48 |

**Three aggravating factors.** The cited source does not produce the number:
`docs/results/operator-position-ranking.md` credits `defence_tables.py`, which

`notebooks/09-placement-search.ipynb` reached this conclusion independently and
states it plainly. The documents had not caught up, and now have.

## A17, the adaptive attacker optimizes the wrong functional

`psbd.evasion.evasion_penalty` hinges on the clean **mean** PSU, while
`psbd.decision.threshold_at_quantile` places the detection threshold at a clean
**quantile**. The attacker and the defender are therefore not looking at the same
statistic, and the attacker is aiming at the one that does not matter.

Simulated, aiming the same attacker at the median instead takes TPR at the 0.25
quantile from **0.999 to 0.080**. Our published adaptive attack is weaker than a
competent one for free, which means **the claim that the defence survives an
adaptive attacker is overstated by however much that gap is worth.**

**The hinge also manufactures the inversion rather than preventing it.** It was
written to stop the attacker overshooting into a regime that would be anomalous in
the other direction. With a noisy k=3 PSU estimate it fires about half the time at
true parity and never in reverse, which is a one sided random walk away from
parity. That is the mechanism behind the measured 0.023 inversion on
`vit_cifar100_badnet_a2o_0_01_evade_l1`, and it means an attacker can produce a
deliberate inversion cheaply, which is the move that breaks a min-rank union.

## A18, a probe at the head is the confidence null

The closed form $\operatorname{tr}\nabla^2_z p_c = 2 p_c(\|p\|^2 - p_c)$ depends
on nothing but the softmax vector, so a probe at the classifier head can carry no
information that free max-softmax confidence does not already have.

Measured on cached baselines, `final_norm_out` against a confidence null that
requires no perturbation at all:

| checkpoint | PSU AUROC | confidence null AUROC | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `vit_cifar100_adaptive_blend_0_01` | 0.751 | 0.754 | 0.003 |
| `vit_cifar100_adaptive_blend_0_05` | 0.514 | 0.515 | 0.001 |
| `vit_cifar100_adaptive_blend_0_1` | 0.456 | 0.457 | 0.001 |

They are the same number. An independent measurement reported the same fact as a
sum to 1.000 across 7 checkpoints under the opposite sign convention.

The explained share of PSU by the softmax vector alone is **0.062 to 0.14 at
input-side probes and 0.956 to 0.992 at `final_norm_out`**, which turns this into
a **probe selection rule a defender can apply without any backdoor data**: compute
that share on clean validation and never deploy a probe whose share is high. It
also converts theory objection 3 from a caveat into a measurement.

A useful corollary, free and defender legal: residualizing PSU on the softmax
vector loses nothing and sometimes gains, 0.886 against 0.862 mean over 6 attacks
at the deployment probe and **+0.121 on adaptive_blend**.

## A21, a confidence-capped backdoor inverts the detector, and our own analysis said it could not

Two attack analyses written in parallel reached opposite conclusions.
`docs/attack-design/A5-low-confidence-backdoor.md` argues a low confidence
backdoor provably cannot beat PSU. `docs/attack-design/cross-defence.md` argues it
can. **A5 is wrong**, and the closed form settles it in 1 calculation.

With the rest of the mass spread uniformly over K - 1 classes,

$$\phi \;\propto\; p_c\big(p_c - \|p\|_2^2\big), \qquad
\|p\|_2^2 = p_c^2 + \frac{(1-p_c)^2}{K-1}$$

is 0 at both $p_c = 1/K$ and $p_c = 1$, with a single peak between them. A5
evaluates the **uniform** endpoint, where the statistic is 0 and the sample does
land on the flagged side. That is true and it is not where an attacker goes.

Measured at K = 100, as a share of the attainable maximum:

| confidence | share of max PSU |
|---|---:|
| attacker capped at 0.60 | **97.0%** |
| clean sample at 0.85 | 73.4% |
| clean sample at 0.95 | **30.6%** |

The peak sits at $p_c = 0.668$. A backdoor held near it carries **3.17 times the
PSU of a clean sample at 0.95**, and PSBD flags **low** PSU as poisoned. Since
**79.3 percent of clean samples sit above $p_c = 0.9$** (measured on our own
caches), such a backdoor is not merely undetected: the ordering reverses and the
detector **inverts**, flagging confident clean inputs instead.

This is the failure mode that also breaks a min-rank probe union, and per A17 our
own evasion penalty already drifts toward producing it accidentally.

**It is cheap and it is published.** LSBA (arXiv:2202.11203) achieves a posterior
cap by a data level relabelling rule at approximately 0 clean accuracy cost, and
reports breaking STRIP, SPECTRE and Spectral Signatures. Whether it breaks us is a
**prediction from our own framework plus their published numbers, not a
measurement**, and LSBA is an unreviewed preprint whose latent family results are
MNIST only. Running it against our detector set is the experiment that decides
whether the H41 defence in depth claim survives.

`A5-low-confidence-backdoor.md` must be corrected before either document is cited.

## Verified and found correct

These were suspected and checked. All 4 are sound and no change was needed.

- **The `post_residual` composition.** `after_attention_residual` replaces a
  block's `forward` while `after_mlp_residual` registers a post hook on the same
  block. An instance attribute `forward` still routes through `Module._call_impl`,
  so hooks still fire. Confirmed: 24 handles attach, rate 0 reproduces the
  unplugged forward exactly, and removing either position changes the output, so
  both mechanisms are live simultaneously.
- **`TokenMask`'s CLS assumption.** Token 0 is the class token at all 3 positions
  the operator is used at. Measured shapes are 197 tokens, being 196 patches plus
  1 CLS, at `after_embedding`, `before_attention_norm` and `mlp_neurons`. Token 0
  survives masking at rate 0.9 while other tokens are dropped.
- **`masked_attention_forward`.** At rate 0 it differs from torchvision's fused
  path by 1.49e-07, which is float32 rounding, not a semantic difference. It
  refuses cross attention rather than computing something plausible and wrong.
- **The cache itself.** 605,927 files across 798 checkpoints checked on 19
  invariants, 0 failures. Every one of the 20,684 rate files storing k identical
  passes sits under `gain_scale` or `scale_up`, where determinism is correct. No
  stochastic operator produced identical passes anywhere, so no silently inert
  probe exists in the tree. The checker was itself validated against a synthetic
  corrupt tree where all 19 checks fired.

## Recovered rather than recomputed

ASR and clean accuracy are deducible from the cached no dropout baseline, which
already stores the model's prediction and the label the loader requested. On the
backdoor split that label is the attack success label, so agreement is the attack
success rate. Cross checked against the 496 checkpoints that carry a training

## A22, the published detection tables were read at the placement-comparison target

Sections 1.3 and 1.4 of `results-report.md` reported `token_mask` at
`before_attention_norm` per dataset and poison rate. Both were read at the
**nearest swept rate to clean-validation shift ratio 0.6**. That is the
matched-strength protocol, whose job is to compare 2 placements at equal
disturbance so the winner is not just whichever perturbs harder. It is not a
detection protocol and answers a different question.

PSBD's own rule is in `4_method.tex:185`: the rate "where the sigma of clean
validation data approach to a high value (0.8 in our experiments)", taking the
smallest rate that reaches it rather than the nearest to it. Nearest is wrong here
twice over, once in the target and once in the direction, and a rate landing just
below the target is one the rule would never have selected.

The cost was large and, worse, uneven, so it did not read as a systematic offset:

| CIFAR-100, TPR@1%FPR | as published (nearest, 0.6) | PSBD's rule (reaching, 0.8) |
|---|---:|---:|
| badnet_a2o 1% | 0.000 | 0.497 |
| badnet_a2o 5% | 0.000 | 0.882 |
| badnet_a2o 10% | 0.004 | 0.997 |
| blend 10% | 0.185 | 1.000 |
| bpp 10% | 0.009 | 0.976 |
| lf 5% | 0.033 | 0.923 |
| lc 1% | 0.038 | 0.003 |

Three cells reading 0.000 to 0.004 for an attack with ASR 1.000 should have been
the tell. A detector at chance on `badnet_a2o` contradicts every other measurement
in the report, and it was recorded rather than questioned.

Note the last row: `lc` moves the other way. Whatever this rule change is, it is
not uniformly favourable, which is the check that it is a protocol correction and
not a selection of the flattering option.

Pooled over the 81-checkpoint ViT panel the same error cost 0.126 TPR@1%FPR
(0.507 against 0.633).
