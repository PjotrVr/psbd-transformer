# SAM audit, 2026-09-11

This is a read only consolidation of everything this project has measured about
sharpness aware minimisation. It cross checks the older H6 verdict against the
fresh matched comparison in `experiments/sam_reading/`, so the paper and the
docs stop citing two different numbers for the same question.

## 1. Inventory of SAM checkpoints and sweeps

`checkpoints/` holds 993 folders with a `_sam_rho_` tag, against 279 plain Adam
combinations of architecture, dataset, attack and poison rate (`checkpoints/`
folder names that carry neither `_sam_rho_` nor `_seed_`).

By architecture: vit 497, swin 496. By dataset: cifar10 248, cifar100 248,
gtsrb 248, tiny 249. No svhn or eurosat SAM checkpoint exists anywhere on disk.
By rho: 0.05, 0.1, 0.15 and 0.2 each have 248 checkpoints, plus a single stray
`vit_tiny_benign_sam_rho_0_5` outside the standard grid. By attack: badnet_a2o,
badnet_a2a, blend, sig, wanet, lf, lc, bpp, adaptive_blend and tact each have 96
checkpoints, plus 33 benign controls.

Training exists for every combination of architecture, dataset, attack and
poison rate the standard grid asks for, at 1 percent and 5 percent, on the 4
datasets SAM was trained on. The gap is entirely svhn and eurosat: 80
combinations (2 architectures times 10 attacks times 2 poison rates) have an
Adam checkpoint and no SAM counterpart, and that is the complete list of what a
matched Adam against SAM comparison still needs at the training stage.

The PSBD sweep has not kept pace with training. Only 512 of the 993 SAM
checkpoints have a `psbd_metrics.json` at all, and of those only 138 swept both
`before_attention_norm_token_mask` (the recommended placement) and
`post_residual` (the published placement), the 2 placements a matched
comparison needs together. Broken down, that coverage is entirely vit (130 of
138, swin 0 of 138), entirely cifar10 and cifar100 (cifar10 61, cifar100 69,
gtsrb 0, tiny 0), and concentrated on lf (24), bpp (23), blend (21), badnet_a2o
(20) and adaptive_blend (18), with sig (3), badnet_a2a (3), lc (10) and wanet
(8) thin and tact at 0.

So the jobs still to submit, in the order that closes the matched comparison
fastest, are: a sweep of both placements over the 512 SAM checkpoints that
already exist but were never analysed for at least one placement, a sweep over
swin entirely (0 of 496 swept for both placements), a sweep over gtsrb and tiny
entirely (0 of 496 swept for both placements between them), and, only after
those, training the 80 svhn and eurosat combinations that have no SAM
checkpoint at all. The first 2 of those 4 reuse checkpoints that already
exist and need no new training, only `cli.sweep` and `cli.analyze`.

## 2. The training recipe against the SAM paper

`training/sam.py` implements the 2 pass update Foret et al. describe: an ascent
step to the worst case point within an l2 ball of radius rho along the
gradient direction, `first_step`, followed by a second backward pass at that
point whose gradient is handed to the base optimizer, `second_step`. Skipping
the second backward and calling `second_step` early raises, by design, because
there is no saved weight to restore. The adaptive (ASAM) variant is
implemented but never turned on in this project, every checkpoint here uses
plain SAM.

`training/loop.py` wraps `torch.optim.Adam` as the base optimizer whenever
`use_sam` is set, `cli/train_backdoor.py --use-sam --rho <value>`, so this
project runs Adam merged with SAM rather than the SGD SAM the original paper
demonstrates. Every run, SAM or not, trains 15 epochs at a constant learning
rate of 1e-4 with weight decay 1e-4, `--lr-schedule cosine` and
`--clip-grad-norm` exist but sit off by default. The rho grid used across all
generators (`pbs/generate_psbd_jobs.py`, `pbs/generate_batched_jobs.py`,
`pbs/generate_overnight_jobs.py`) is 0.05, 0.1, 0.15 and 0.2, which sits inside
the 0.05 to 0.5 range the SAM paper reports as working for their own
experiments, per the citation already recorded in H6.

2 departures from the reference are worth stating plainly. First, this
project runs Adam under the hood rather than SGD, and the SAM paper's own
derivation of why sharpness aware training helps is a first order SGD
expansion, so nothing here inherits that argument automatically. Second, the
detection paper that motivates this whole line of enquiry, Zhang et al.
(`papers/reliable_poisoned_sample_detection_against_backdoor_attacks_enhanced_by_sharpness_aware_minimization/`,
arXiv 2411.11525), adds a feature scaling stage after SAM training
specifically to undo the rise in clean feature variance SAM causes. This
project never implements that stage, so every SAM checkpoint here is closer to
their own "SAM without feature scaling" ablation row than to their headline
numbers, and that row is substantially weaker in their own table.

The 2 findings that disagree are the first and the fourth. The naive
aggregate reports a negligible, statistically unexamined +0.009 and treats SAM
as dropped, while the matched `sam_reading` comparison reports a positive
delta for the token mask placement whose interval excludes 0 at rho 0.1, and a
negative delta for the published placement whose interval also excludes 0 at
rho 0.15 and 0.2. `sam_reading` is the more trustworthy of the 2, for 3
reasons stated in its own README and confirmed by H19. It matches on
architecture, dataset, attack and poison rate rather than pooling checkpoints
that were swept over different, unequal grids. It separates the 2
placements instead of averaging over whichever one a given checkpoint
happened to have been swept at, which is exactly the confound H19 traces the
sign flip to. And it reports a bootstrap interval rather than a bare mean, so
"SAM helps" and "SAM does not matter" can be told apart from "the sample size
says nothing either way." The +0.009 number should be treated as superseded
wherever it still appears, in `docs/results-report.md` section 4.7,
`docs/hypothesis/H6-sam-improves-detectability.md`'s dropped verdict, and
`docs/seed-replication-plan.md`'s struck row, none of which have been updated
since `sam_reading` landed on 2026-09-11.

That said, `sam_reading`'s own README states its coverage plainly: 24 matched
combinations per rho, vit only, cifar10 and cifar100 only, no swin, gtsrb or
tiny. So the corrected finding settles the question for that architecture and
those 2 datasets, and the older, broader sounding "SAM is out of scope" verdict
should not be read as covering swin, gtsrb or tiny at all, since no matched
comparison exists there yet on either side of the argument.

## 4. Hypotheses for why SAM should or should not help a prediction shift detector

**SAM sharpens the loss landscape near clean inputs but not near poisoned
ones, so PSBD's clean vs poisoned PSU gap should widen.** The mechanism
argument, drawn directly from the SAM paper's own stage 2 motivation, cuts the
other way for a feature space detector but has not been shown to cut either
way for a prediction space one until `sam_reading` measured it directly.
Evidence for: the corrected token mask delta is positive at every rho, with
2 of 4 intervals excluding 0. Evidence against: the delta is not
monotonic in rho (it peaks at rho 0.1 and falls at 0.15 before rising again at
0.2), which a clean landscape sharpening story would not obviously predict.

**SAM raises clean feature variance, which should raise clean PSU and close
the gap PSU thresholds on, since PSBD has no feature scaling stage to cancel
it.** This is the mechanism H6 proposed and it is well supported for the
published placement. Evidence for: residual dropout's AUROC falls under SAM
at every rho measured, with 2 of 4 rho values excluding 0 in the negative
direction, and `sam_backdoor_effect` measured the underlying clean intra class
variance rise directly (+0.02 to +0.31 across 4 attacks). Evidence against:
the token mask placement does not show the same fall, so if rising clean
variance is the mechanism, it must interact with the choice of what is
perturbed and where, not act as a placement independent tax on every probe.

**SAM amplifies the backdoor's causal footprint (TAC, direction norm) without
increasing how separable clean and triggered representations are, so a
detector that reads the representation gains nothing while a detector that
reads a label decision might still gain something.** This is the account H6's
positive control settled for feature space detectors, silhouette barely moves
while TAC roughly doubles, and it is consistent with, though not proven by,
the token mask placement's small positive delta, since token mask reads a
decision rather than a feature vector.

**SAM relocates the backdoor to an earlier block without weakening it, so a
probe placed at a block band tuned for Adam checkpoints could be reading the
wrong depth under SAM.** H16 measured this directly, peak layer moves earlier
monotonically in rho for every attack, 12 to 10 or 11 to 8 depending on the
attack, while the benign control's peak does not move at all. This predicts
that block band restricted placements (`docs/hypothesis/H10-depth-band-placement.md`)
should be more sensitive to SAM than the block spanning placements this
project favours, a prediction nobody has tested, since block band sweeps and
SAM sweeps have essentially no combination in common on disk (checked directly,
0 combinations of the 2).

**WaNet's implantation is unstable under SAM independent of detection, so any
aggregate that includes WaNet at low poison rates conflates "SAM hurts
detection" with "SAM broke the attack before detection could matter."** This
is a confound rather than a finding about detection, recorded once
(`docs/results/detection-operating-points.md`, ASR 0.650 to 0.007 on cifar100
5 percent). It has not been checked against the corrected `sam_reading` grid,
whose combination list includes wanet, so this is a live risk in the current
24 combination count rather than a settled one.

## 5. Measurements still needed, ranked

1. **Sweep both placements over the 512 SAM checkpoints with a
   `psbd_metrics.json` but missing one of the 2 placements, then extend to
   gtsrb and tiny where the sweep has not started at all.** This is the
   cheapest item on the list because most of the training and part of the
   analysis already exist. Script: `python -m cli.sweep --position
   before_attention_norm --operator token_mask` and the equivalent
   `post_residual` dropout call, per folder, followed by `python -m
   cli.analyze`. Cost: CPU only for `cli.analyze` on cached passes, GPU time
   for `cli.sweep` scales with however many of the 512 lack one placement,
   roughly proportional to 1 forward pass sweep per missing placement per
   checkpoint.

2. **Sweep swin entirely for the matched comparison, both placements.** 0 of
   496 swin SAM checkpoints currently have both placements swept, so this is
   the single largest gap in the corrected finding's generality, and the
   dataset priority in this project (cifar100 and tiny as primary, per
   feedback on file) means the tiny half of this gap outranks the cifar10 half
   already covered. Script: `python -m cli.sweep` over the swin SAM folders,
   `pbs/generate_psbd_jobs.py` already knows the SAM folder naming convention
   and can be pointed at swin. Cost: GPU, comparable to the vit sweep already
   run, since swin and vit sweeps have historically cost about the same per
   checkpoint.

3. **Check whether wanet's known SAM instability (ASR collapsing on cifar100
   5 percent) appears anywhere in the 24 combinations `sam_reading` already
   covers, and exclude or flag those rows if so.** This does not need a new
   run, it needs reading `results/_experiments/sam_reading/sam_reading.json`
   for the wanet rows' ASR alongside their AUROC delta, since an attack that
   stopped implanting cannot be said to be more or less detectable. Script:
   a short filter over the existing JSON, no GPU.

4. **Retrain 1 additional SAM seed per attack at rho 0.1 on vit cifar10, to
   test whether H16's "SAM relocates the backdoor" finding is a property of
   SAM or a property of retraining in general.** H16 names this as its
   cheapest and most important open follow up, since only seed 0 exists for
   every SAM checkpoint on disk and the relocation claim cannot currently be
   told apart from ordinary seed to seed variation. Script:
   `python -m cli.train_backdoor --use-sam --rho 0.1 --seed 1`, one run per
   attack already in H16's set (badnet_a2o, blend, bpp, lf), followed by
   `experiments/backdoor_neurons/measure.py`. Cost: 4 training runs plus 4
   feature extraction passes, no sweep needed.

5. **Train the 80 missing svhn and eurosat combinations at 1 and 5 percent, on
   both architectures, across the 10 attacks in scope.** This is the most
   expensive item and the lowest priority, both because svhn and eurosat are
   not the dataset priority in this project and because every placement level
   question above can be answered first on the 4 datasets that already have
   SAM checkpoints. Script: `pbs/generate_batched_jobs.py`, which already
   knows the SAM folder naming convention, pointed at the 2 missing datasets.
   Cost: 80 training runs at 15 epochs each, the largest single item here by
   GPU hours, followed by the same 2 stage PSBD sweep as every other
   checkpoint.

6. **Rerun `experiments/sam_backdoor_effect/measure.py` on lc and sig, the 2
   clean label attacks, at their respective rate caps.** The existing
   amplification measurement (TAC, silhouette, clean intra class variance)
   covers badnet_a2o, blend, bpp and lf only, so the mechanism account in
   section 4 above has no direct evidence for or against the 2 attacks this
   project's own feedback flags as hard baselines worth judging detection on.
   Script: `python -m experiments.sam_backdoor_effect.measure --attack lc sig
   --poison-tag <the capped rate tag for each>`. Cost: GPU, feature
   extraction only, comparable to the existing 4 attack run.
