\section{Latent analysis}
\label{sec:latent}

\begin{claim}{STRONG}{F12}
If the backdoor in a ViT is a single linear direction in the residual stream, written late and routed through attention from the trigger's patches to the class token, then removing that direction alone should kill the attack while zeroing any set of coordinates should not, the direction's alignment to its final form should assemble over a band of layers rather than persist from the start, and patching the trigger's own tokens with their clean values should restore the clean answer fully before the routing depth and not after. Each is tested below, and each states whether it rests on a single checkpoint or on a bootstrap over the panel.
\end{claim}

\subsection{One direction, not a set of neurons}

\Cref{tab:direction-ablation} is the causal test, on \AblationCheckpoints{} CIFAR-10 checkpoints at the highest panel rate, a checkpoint per attack\finding{STRONG}{F12}. Projecting out the lowest-rank backdoor direction after the final LayerNorm, where the head reads, leaves attack success at most \AblationDirectionMaxAsr{} on the all-to-one attacks, while a random direction leaves it at \AblationRandomDirectionMinAsr{} at the lowest and zeroing the most trigger-activated coordinates leaves it at \AblationTopCoordinatesMinAsr{} at the lowest. The direction is real, causal and rotated off the coordinate basis, which is why every earlier attempt of this project to exploit units, heads and coordinate rankings failed and why a structured mask cannot beat unstructured noise on the operator axis. \Cref{fig:direction} gives the depth profile on the same checkpoints, relative direction norm, mean trigger-activated change and clean-versus-triggered CKA per layer, with the benign reference probed by the same trigger peaking at \DirectionBenignPeakRelNorm{} against a smallest final value of \DirectionBackdooredMinFinalRelNorm{} on the backdoored ones. This is a single dataset and a single checkpoint per attack, with a benign control and the ablation's own controls.

\subsection{Crystallisation and the per-layer TAC pass}

The direction does not persist uniformly\finding{SUPPORTING}{F13}. \Cref{tab:direction-crystallisation} reads, on \CrystallisationCheckpoints{} CIFAR-100 and Tiny checkpoints across every poison rate trained, the first layer at which a layer's direction aligns at least half with the final one. It is \CrystallisationBadnetLayer{} for BadNets and \CrystallisationBlendLayer{} for Blend, with the poison rate moving neither, so the global trigger crystallises earlier than the patch. \Cref{tab:tac-layers} and \cref{fig:tac-layers} extend the depth profile to \TacLayersCheckpoints{} checkpoints on GTSRB, CIFAR-100 and Tiny with a GPU pass run for this paper on \TacLayersSamples{} paired images each, and the onset layer, where the relative norm first reaches half its final value, is \TacLayersBadnetOnset{} for BadNets and \TacLayersBlendOnset{} for Blend on average, with the largest benign peak at \TacLayersBenignPeakMax{}. The earlier CIFAR-10 ordering, distributed triggers onset early and the patch trigger late, holds on CIFAR-100 and Tiny and reverses on GTSRB, where Blend's onset is later than BadNets', so the onset ordering is a property of the dataset as well as of the trigger and the per-attack band rule that was built on it is not recoverable from this pass either.

\subsection{Activation patching}

\Cref{tab:activation-patching} and \cref{fig:patching} average, over every cached cell of each attack, the recovery of the clean answer when a token group at a layer of the residual stream is overwritten with its value on the same image without the trigger, the paired counterfactual \citet{zhang2024patching} recommend over noise corruption\finding{STRONG}{F14}. For the local triggers, BadNets and TaCT, patching the trigger's own tokens restores the clean answer at \PatchingLocalEarlyTrigger{} over layers \PatchingEarlyLayers{}, the tokens alone still suffice at layer \PatchingBadnetLastFull{} for BadNets, the class token first recovers more than the trigger tokens at layer \PatchingBadnetCrossover{}, and a random token group of the same count recovers at most \PatchingRandomNullMax{} anywhere on the global attacks and at most a few thousandths on the local ones. The null is the qualification. A global trigger covers every patch, so its trigger set is all \VitPatches{} tokens and a same-count random group is the same set, which is why the global rows read the same on the null and why the patching result is a local-trigger result on \PatchingCells{} cells over \PanelDatasetsCached{} datasets. On those, the backdoor is causally located entirely in its own patches through the first half of the network and reaches the class token only in the last quarter.

\subsection{Routing, sinks and artifact tokens}

\Cref{tab:routing} and \cref{fig:routing} consolidate the routing records\finding{SUPPORTING}{F15}. On triggered images the class token's attention mass on BadNets' trigger tokens is \RoutingBadnetClsEarly{} over blocks \BandEarly{}, \RoutingBadnetClsRouting{} over blocks \BandMiddle{} and \RoutingBadnetClsLate{} over blocks \BandLate{}, against \RoutingBenignClsLate{} late on the benign reference, and the global triggers read \RoutingGlobalClsRouting{} on the middle band because their trigger set is every patch. Attention's share of the summed target-logit attribution of the sublayers' writes is \RoutingBadnetAttentionShare{} for BadNets, so on the logit the attention write dominates for the patch trigger, and the MLP being token-wise cannot move content between tokens whatever it writes. The trigger's tokens carry a norm rank of \RoutingLocalRankRouting{} among patches over the middle band for the local triggers, against \RoutingBenignRankRouting{} on the benign reference under the same trigger, which is the raw pixel patch still being visible there and is why the first blocks are excluded from every norm statistic, and the peak max-over-median patch-norm ratio from the third block on is \RoutingBadnetRatioPeak{} for BadNets against \RoutingBenignRatioPeak{} on clean images. When the trigger's tokens are masked the class token's attention mass is restored by other tokens at a takeover fraction of \RoutingBadnetTakeover{} over the middle band for BadNets against \RoutingBenignTakeover{} on the benign reference, so the trigger's role is one other tokens step into only partly. This ViT has no native register population, the local trigger manufactures one and the global triggers do not, and the artifact-token detector built on the norm ratio is a single-cell observation under audit rather than a result.

\subsection{Prediction depth}

Prediction depth \citep{baldock2021difficulty} reads every block through the network's own final LayerNorm and head and averages the lens probability of the final answer over depth, a single forward pass and no rate to pick\finding{STRONG}{F17}. \Cref{tab:prediction-depth} pairs it against the recommended placement on all \DepthCells{} cached cells. Its AUROC is \DepthAurocAll{}, a paired delta of \DepthMinusPsbdAuroc{} with interval \DepthMinusPsbdAurocCi{}, and its TPR at the \LowestQuantilePercent{} budget is \DepthMinusPsbdTpr{} above PSBD's with interval \DepthMinusPsbdTprCi{}, winning on \DepthTprWins{} cells. The gain is concentrated on the hard attacks, \DepthMinusPsbdTprHard{} at the low budget with interval \DepthMinusPsbdTprHardCi{}, and it loses on BadNets at every rate, so the earlier reading of a large low-budget advantage held on the subset it was read on and does not on the panel. The benign references read \DepthBenignAuroc{}. A statistic with no perturbation at all reaching about the same AUROC as the best perturbation says that what PSBD measures is a property of the sample's trajectory through depth as much as of its robustness, and the pair are complementary at the operating point PSBD is weakest at.
