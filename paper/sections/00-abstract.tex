\begin{abstract}
Prediction Shift Backdoor Detection (PSBD) flags a poisoned input by how little its prediction moves when the network is perturbed with dropout at inference. It was designed for ResNets, where dropout on the residual stream is the natural perturbation. We adapt it to Vision Transformers (ViTs), where a block offers several sites and several kinds of perturbation, and we show that this choice decides the outcome. Masking whole tokens at the input of every attention block reaches a mean AUROC of 0.935 over 65 backdoored ViT-B/16 models spanning 4 datasets, 7 attacks and poison rates from 1\% to 10\%, flags 0.629 of triggered inputs at 1\% false positives, and reaches 0.968 on Swin-S. Dropout on the residual stream, the ResNet site, reaches 0.832 on the same models, and the gap is widest at 1\% poisoning. We explain the ranking by following the backdoor through the network. It is 1 direction in the residual stream, written in the last third of the network and routed from the trigger's tokens to the class token through attention, so removing tokens before attention removes the shortcut, while noise at the same site is absorbed by the LayerNorm behind it. We also report the limits. An attacker who trains against 1 probe defeats it, a union of probes restores detection, and all-to-all attacks remain open.
\end{abstract}
