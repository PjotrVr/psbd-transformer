\section{Adapting PSBD to a transformer}
\label{sec:method}

We keep PSBD's decision rule fixed and vary only what is injected and where. This keeps every placement comparable and keeps the method faithful to the paper in everything that is not architecture-specific.

\paragraph{Positions and operators.}
A placement is a position paired with an operator. A position is a named tensor boundary inside every block, attached through forward hooks so that the model's own dropout modules stay off and no trained weight is touched. The basis holds 9 single positions (the attention input before and after its LayerNorm, the MLP input before and after its LayerNorm, the 2 branch outputs before their residual adds, the 2 residual streams after the adds, and the embedding), 2 structured positions (the attention heads and the MLP hidden units), and 4 positions ported from other detectors (the input pixels and the LayerNorm outputs). An operator is what the hook does to the tensor: dropout as in the paper, token masking (zero whole tokens with the class token protected, then rescale), channel masking (zero whole channels), isotropic Gaussian noise scaled to the tensor's standard deviation, and gain scaling of a LayerNorm output. Every operator takes 1 rate $p$, and every placement is swept over a ladder of rates from 0.05 to 0.9.

\paragraph{The statistic.}
We use the fractional form of the statistic, the drop in the predicted class's probability divided by that probability without the perturbation, so that a confident and an unconfident clean prediction that lose the same share of their probability score alike. The decision is one-sided, low means poisoned, exactly as in the paper.

\paragraph{The rate rule and the comparison rule.}
The paper picks the dropout rate at which the clean validation set's shift ratio reaches 0.8. We keep that rule for deployment and call it the adaptive rule. It is not usable for comparing placements, because the same nominal rate is a different disturbance at different sites: masking 30\% of the tokens before attention and dropping 30\% of the residual stream's coordinates move the prediction by very different amounts. So every comparison between placements is also read at a matched rule: the rate at which each placement first moves 60\% of clean validation predictions. At a matched shift ratio 2 placements have been given the same budget of disturbance, and what remains is how well that disturbance separates poisoned from clean inputs. We report both, and note when they disagree.

\paragraph{The threshold and the metrics.}
The threshold is a quantile of the clean validation set's scores, so the false-positive rate is set by the defender. We report the area under the ROC curve at the paper's 25th-percentile threshold and the true-positive rate at the 1\%, 10\% and 25\% quantiles. The clean and triggered test sets are paired: every triggered image is the triggered copy of an image in the clean set, so the 2 populations differ only by the trigger.

\paragraph{Number of passes.}
We use $k = 3$ passes as the paper does. \Cref{sec:robustness:passes} measures what more passes buy.
