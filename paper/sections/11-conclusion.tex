\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

PSBD transfers to ViTs once its perturbation is placed where a ViT carries a backdoor. Masking whole tokens at the input of every attention block detects poisoned inputs with a mean AUROC of 0.935 over 65 backdoored ViT-B/16 models and 0.968 over 53 Swin-S models, against 0.832 and 0.871 for dropout after the residual add, the site the original paper used. The gain is largest at 1\% poisoning, where a detector is needed most.

The placement is not a tuning accident. A backdoor in a ViT is 1 direction in the residual stream, written in the last third of the network and routed through attention from the trigger's patch tokens to the class token. A perturbation that removes tokens before attention removes the shortcut, while a perturbation on the residual stream perturbs the decision itself. The same account explains why noise and masking swap places between the attention input and the MLP input, predicts which attacks show the prediction shift toward the target class that the original paper observed, and reproduces the direction removal of Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al.\ once the direction is removed from every residual write.

For a practitioner the recommendation is short. On a ViT or a Swin, run PSBD with token masking at the attention input and choose the rate by the clean shift ratio. Against a knowledgeable attacker, combine several placements, since an attacker who evades 1 probe does not evade the others. The union of probes costs a few forward passes per input and restores detection above 0.97 on every attack we trained against a single probe.

3 questions remain open. An attacker who trains against the union of probes, a like-for-like comparison to other input-level detectors on the hard attacks, and the all-to-all attack, whose backdoor has no single direction and which, on the present evidence, needs a different statistic.
