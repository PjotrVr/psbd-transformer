\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The results above are read on 65 ViT-B/16 and 53 Swin-S models fine-tuned from ImageNet weights for 15 epochs at 224 pixels, on datasets of small images. We state the boundaries of that evidence and the cases where the method falls short, since a deployer needs them as much as the gains.

\paragraph{All-to-all attacks.}
PSBD does poorly on BadNets in its all-to-all form, where every class is mapped to the next one. PSBD-TM reads a mean AUROC of 0.411 over the 12 all-to-all models against 0.891 for the all-to-one form. The direction analysis of \cref{sec:mechanism} suggests why. An all-to-all backdoor has no single direction, since each source class pushes toward a different target and the pushes partly cancel, so the shortcut is not the firm single decision the statistic relies on. An entropy statistic over the perturbed predictions recovers 0.761 on these models and is a plausible route, which we leave for future work.

\paragraph{SIG and WaNet on CIFAR-10.}
PSBD-TM's 2 weakest readings are SIG and WaNet on CIFAR-10 at 10\% poisoning, at 0.418 and 0.459. SIG's shift ratio rises quickly with the mask rate, so the 0.8 target lands on a rate that disturbs clean predictions as much as poisoned ones, and a lower target repairs it. WaNet on CIFAR-10 is the 1 configuration PSBD-TM does not handle at any rate we tried.

\paragraph{Adaptive-Blend and the clean-label attacks.}
Adaptive-Blend, the attack in our set built to evade input-level detectors, never reaches the attack success bar on any dataset or poison rate we trained, so none of the detection numbers include it. A clean-label attack can poison only the target class, so its reachable rate is that class's share of the training set, which is 10\% on CIFAR-10, 1\% on CIFAR-100, 0.5\% on Tiny ImageNet and 5.6\% on GTSRB at the largest class. Within those ceilings SIG implants only on CIFAR-10 at 10\% and on SVHN and EuroSAT, and Label-Consistent only on GTSRB at $\epsilon = 16/255$. The clean-label evidence is therefore thin, and the SVHN and EuroSAT detection sweeps are in progress. This is a property of the attacks on these datasets rather than of the detector.

\paragraph{Seeds and coverage.}
Most backdoored models have 1 training seed, and every gain is a paired difference with a bootstrap interval over models rather than over seeds. On the 14 models with 3 seeds PSBD-TM moves by 0.012 AUROC between seeds (\cref{sec:robustness:seeds}), which is small against every gain we report, and 2 further seeds of every model are being trained. The Swin evaluation covers 3 datasets and 5 placements. The comparison to other detectors is a smoke test on 3 models until the full run completes. 13 GTSRB training runs collapsed in their last epochs under the constant learning rate. They are excluded, and their reruns use a cosine schedule.

\paragraph{Sharpness-aware training.}
A line of work trains the victim with sharpness-aware minimisation (SAM) \cite{foret2021sam} so that backdoor neurons stand out more and poisoned-sample detectors improve \cite{zhang2024samdetection}. We trained SAM versions of the BadNets, Blend, BPP, LF and WaNet models at sharpness radii from 0.05 to 0.2 on both architectures, 24 matched pairs per radius, and \cref{app:sam} reads PSBD on them. SAM raises PSBD-TM's AUROC by +0.02 to +0.04 depending on the radius, an interval that excludes 0 only at radius 0.1, and it lowers PSBD-RD's by 0.04 to 0.08. The gain for PSBD-TM is real but small against the effect of the placement, so we report it and do not build on it.

\paragraph{Adaptive attackers.}
\Cref{sec:robustness:adaptive} shows that a single probe can be defeated and that the union of probes is not defeated by the same attacker. An attacker who trains against the union is untested and is the natural next step, as is a trigger designed to survive token masking.
