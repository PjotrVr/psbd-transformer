\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The results above are read on 65 ViT-B/16 and 53 Swin-S models fine-tuned from ImageNet weights for 15 epochs at 224 pixels, on datasets of small images. We state the boundaries of that evidence and the cases where the method falls short, since a deployer needs them as much as the gains.

\paragraph{All-to-all attacks.}
PSBD does poorly on BadNets in its all-to-all form, where every class is mapped to the next one. Attention-input token masking reads a mean AUROC of 0.411 over the 12 all-to-all models against 0.891 for the all-to-one form. The direction analysis of \cref{sec:mechanism} suggests why. An all-to-all backdoor has no single direction, since each source class pushes toward a different target and the pushes partly cancel, so the shortcut is not the firm single decision the statistic relies on. An entropy statistic over the perturbed predictions recovers 0.761 on these models and is a plausible route, which we leave for future work.

\paragraph{The 2 failing models.}
CIFAR-10 SIG and WaNet at 10\% poisoning score below chance under attention-input token masking at the adaptive rule. SIG reads 0.878 at the matched rule, so its failure is the 0.8 shift target overshooting for a model whose predictions move easily, and a lower target repairs it. WaNet on CIFAR-10 fails under both rules and is the 1 configuration among the 65 that token masking does not handle.

\paragraph{Clean-label attacks.}
A clean-label attack can poison only the target class, so its reachable rate is that class's share of the training set. That is 10\% on CIFAR-10, 1\% on CIFAR-100, 0.5\% on Tiny ImageNet and 5.6\% on GTSRB at the largest class. Within those ceilings SIG never clears the success bar on GTSRB, with a mean attack success of 0.72 over 5 seeds at 5\%, and Label-Consistent clears only on GTSRB at $\epsilon = 16/255$ and on CIFAR-10 at a clean-accuracy cost of 9 points. The clean-label coverage therefore rests on SVHN and EuroSAT, where SIG implants at 5\% and 10\% with 3 seeds each and whose detection sweeps are in progress. This is a property of the attacks on these datasets rather than of the detector.

\paragraph{Seeds and coverage.}
Most backdoored models have 1 training seed, and every gain is a paired difference with a bootstrap interval over models rather than over seeds. On the 14 models with 3 seeds attention-input token masking moves by 0.012 AUROC between seeds (\cref{sec:robustness:seeds}), which is small against every gain we report, and further seeds are being trained. The Swin evaluation covers 3 datasets and 5 placements, since no attack implants on Tiny ImageNet on Swin at the rates we tried. The comparison to other detectors is a smoke test on 3 models until the full run completes. 13 GTSRB training runs collapsed in their last epochs under the constant learning rate. They are excluded, and their reruns use a cosine schedule.

\paragraph{Sharpness-aware training.}
A line of work trains the victim with sharpness-aware minimisation so that backdoor neurons stand out more and poisoned-sample detectors improve. We trained SAM versions of the BadNets, Blend, BPP, LF and WaNet models at sharpness radii from 0.05 to 0.5 on both architectures. SAM does amplify the backdoor by the 3 metrics that paper uses, but PSBD's AUROC on the SAM-trained models is within about 0.01 of the Adam-trained ones on the models that hold both versions, so we do not recommend it as a companion to PSBD on ViTs and report it as a null result.

\paragraph{Adaptive attackers.}
\Cref{sec:robustness:adaptive} shows that a single probe can be defeated and that the union of probes is not defeated by the same attacker. An attacker who trains against the union is untested and is the natural next step, as is a trigger designed to survive token masking.
