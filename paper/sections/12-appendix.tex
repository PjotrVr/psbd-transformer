\section{Detection results per poison rate}
\label{app:results}

\Cref{tab:results-rate-1,tab:results-rate-5,tab:results-rate-10} give the full detection results at 1\%, 5\% and 10\% poisoning, 1 row per backdoored model, grouped by dataset. ASR is the attack success rate of the model, the share of triggered non-target images classified as the target, and CA is its clean accuracy on the test set. The 2 blocks of detection columns are PSBD-TM (token masking at the attention input) and PSBD-RD (dropout on the residual stream after both adds). AUROC is the one-sided area under the ROC curve, never flipped, so a value below 0.5 means the statistic ordered poisoned and clean inputs the wrong way round on that model. TPR@FPR is the share of triggered inputs flagged when the threshold is set on the 2000 clean validation images so that 10\% or 20\% of them are flagged.

A model whose attack success is below 0.85 prints its ASR and CA but no detection numbers, since a detector cannot be credited or blamed for a backdoor that was never planted. Reading the 3 tables together shows what the evaluation can and cannot say. BadNets, Blend, BPP and LF implant at every rate on every dataset. TaCT implants everywhere except GTSRB at 1\%. WaNet implants only at 5\% and 10\% and not on CIFAR-100, SIG only on CIFAR-10 at 10\% and on SVHN and EuroSAT, and Adaptive-Blend never reaches the bar. \Cref{tab:results-benign} gives the clean accuracy of the benign reference model of each dataset, trained with the same recipe, which every clean-accuracy drop in the paper subtracts against.

\section{The placement basis}
\label{app:basis}

\Cref{tab:basis-declaration} lists the placements every model was swept over. A placement is a site (where the perturbation is injected, \cref{fig:vit-block}) paired with a perturbation (what the hook does), optionally restricted to a band of blocks. The family column groups sites by where they sit relative to the residual stream. Input-side sites perturb a sublayer's input before its LayerNorm. Residual-adjacent sites perturb a branch output just before its residual add or the stream just after it. The structured sites perturb attention heads and MLP hidden units, and the ported sites come from other detectors, scaling input pixels as SCALE-UP does and scaling a LayerNorm's gain as IBD-PSC does. The rates column is the ladder each placement was swept over. Masks and dropout use a share of the tensor, Gaussian noise a multiple of the tensor's standard deviation. The last 6 entries were added after the first 18 had been read, to fill \cref{tab:staircase-residual,tab:staircase-dropout-sites,tab:staircase-bands}, and their sweeps are in progress.

\section{Selection protocol}
\label{app:protocol}

Choosing the best of 18 placements on the models it is later reported on would give an optimistic reading, so the basis declares a split before any sweep. Placements are selected on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB and reported on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet. On the 32 held-out CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet models PSBD-TM reads a mean AUROC of 0.944, above its mean of 0.935 over all models, so its lead is not a product of the selection. The numbers in the main text pool both halves.

\section{The shift ratio target}
\label{app:rate}

The original rule reads each placement at the rate where 80\% of clean validation predictions change. \Cref{fig:shift-ladder} checks that target on our models by interpolating every model's rate ladder onto a shared grid of achieved shift ratios from 0.2 to 0.98 and reading AUROC and TPR along it, for all models and separately for the models at each poison rate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_shift_ladder.pdf}
\caption{Mean AUROC, TPR at 1\% FPR and TPR at 10\% FPR of PSBD-TM against the achieved clean shift ratio. The dashed line marks the target of 0.8. Above 0.8 fewer models reach the target within the swept rate ladder and the mean is taken over those that do.}
\label{fig:shift-ladder}
\end{figure}

AUROC is flat above a shift ratio of about 0.6, at 0.927 at 0.6 and 0.936 at 0.8, while the true-positive rate at a fixed false-positive budget keeps rising with the target. At 1\% FPR it goes from 0.408 at 0.6 to 0.568 at 0.8 and 0.646 at 0.9. A higher target therefore buys operating-point performance without moving the ranking statistic. The caveat is that only 52 of the 65 models reach a shift ratio of 0.9 within the swept rate ladder, so the means past 0.8 are over a shrinking subset. We keep the original target of 0.8, which sits at the knee of every curve.

\Cref{tab:tac-layers} gives, for each of the 18 models in \cref{fig:tac-layers}, the block at which the backdoor direction's norm relative to the clean activation peaks, that peak value, the first block at which the relative norm reaches half its final value (the onset), the final-layer linear centered kernel alignment (CKA) \cite{kornblith2019cka} between clean and triggered activations (1.0 means the trigger leaves the representation unchanged), and the mean absolute trigger-activated change (TAC) per dimension at the final layer. A source-specific attack changes the representation of the few source classes only, so a direction averaged over all classes is small even though the attack succeeds.

\section{Comparison with Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al. in detail}
\label{app:picek}

Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al.~\cite{karayalcin2026backdoor} remove a backdoor by projecting the direction of 1 selected layer out of every weight matrix that writes to the residual stream, the patch embedding and all 24 attention and MLP output projections, and report attack success falling from 97.7 to 6.7 on average with Blend on CIFAR-100 the 1 survivor. \Cref{tab:erasure} repeats their procedure on our models, with their layer selection rule, and adds 2 controls, the same edit restricted to blocks 10 and 11 and the same edit with a random direction of the same norm.

\IfFileExists{tables/erasure.tex}{\input{tables/erasure}}{}
\FloatBarrier

The removal reproduces. Every attack except Blend on CIFAR-100 falls to an attack success at or below 0.034 at a clean-accuracy cost of 0.4 to 9.5 points, Blend on CIFAR-100 stays at 1.000 at both poison rates exactly as they report, and neither control moves any attack. Our first attempt had edited only the last 2 blocks and left every attack intact, which the blocks 10 and 11 column now shows to be expected. The direction is re-created by every block, so removing it means removing it everywhere.

Their second claim, that distributed triggers reach the class token earlier than patch triggers, is measured independently in \cref{fig:tac-layers}. It holds on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, where the onset of the backdoor direction comes earlier for Blend, BPP and LF than for BadNets, and it reverses on GTSRB, which they did not run.

Their third contribution is a data-free detector that scores the classifier head's rows against the early layers' output projections and flags a model when 1 class stands out by more than 3 standard deviations, and it is reported to flag WaNet and BPP reliably and patch triggers never. We ran the rule with their grid over the number of layers and the floor of the standard deviation on 15 of our backdoored models and 4 benign ones. It names the correct target on all 5 WaNet models, with a best $Z$ of 35 to 139, and on BPP only on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet it names a wrong class at every poison rate, it never fires on BadNets, Blend or TaCT, and it raises a false alarm on 3 to 15\% of the grid on every benign model. Their WaNet result reproduces, their BPP result does not on 2 of 3 datasets, and \cref{tab:direction} says why a weight-space rule reads little. The backdoor is a rotated direction, not a coordinate pattern, and a score built from coordinates of the head and the weights sees only its projection.
