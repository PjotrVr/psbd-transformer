\section{Detection results per poison rate}
\label{app:results}

\Cref{tab:results-rate-1,tab:results-rate-5,tab:results-rate-10} give the full detection results at 1\%, 5\% and 10\% poisoning, 1 row per backdoored model, grouped by dataset. ASR is the attack success rate of the model, the share of triggered non-target images classified as the target, and CA is its clean accuracy on the test set. The 2 blocks of detection columns are PSBD-TM (token masking at the attention input) and PSBD-RD (dropout on the residual stream after both adds). AUROC is the one-sided area under the ROC curve, never flipped, so a value below 0.5 means the statistic ordered poisoned and clean inputs the wrong way round on that model. TPR@FPR is the share of triggered inputs flagged when the threshold is set on the 2000 clean validation images so that 10\% or 20\% of them are flagged.

A model whose attack success is below \AsrBar{} prints its ASR and CA but no detection numbers, since a detector cannot be credited or blamed for a backdoor that was never planted. Reading the 3 tables together shows what the evaluation can and cannot say. BadNets, Blend, BPP and LF implant at every rate on every dataset. TaCT implants everywhere except GTSRB at 1\%. WaNet implants only at 5\% and 10\% and not on CIFAR-100, SIG only on CIFAR-10 at 10\% and on SVHN and EuroSAT, and Adaptive-Blend never reaches the bar. \Cref{tab:results-benign} gives the clean accuracy of the benign reference model of each dataset, trained with the same recipe, which every clean-accuracy drop in the paper subtracts against.

\section{The placement basis}
\label{app:basis}

\Cref{tab:basis-declaration} lists the placements every model was swept over. A placement is a site (where the perturbation is injected, \cref{fig:vit-block}) paired with a perturbation (what the hook does), optionally restricted to a band of blocks. The family column groups sites by where they sit relative to the residual stream. Input-side sites perturb a sublayer's input before its LayerNorm. Residual-adjacent sites perturb a branch output just before its residual add or the stream just after it. The structured sites perturb attention heads and MLP hidden units, and the ported sites come from other detectors, scaling input pixels as SCALE-UP does and scaling a LayerNorm's gain as IBD-PSC does. The rates column is the ladder each placement was swept over. Masks and dropout use a share of the tensor, Gaussian noise a multiple of the tensor's standard deviation. The last entries were added after the first had been read, to fill \cref{tab:staircase-residual,tab:staircase-dropout-sites,tab:staircase-bands}. All \BasisSize{} declared placements now reach the adaptive shift target somewhere on the panel. Coverage is still uneven across them, so every claim about the basis is restricted to the placements common to every compared cell.

\section{Selection protocol}
\label{app:protocol}

Choosing the best placement on the models it is later reported on would give an optimistic reading, so the basis declares a split before any sweep. Placements are selected on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB and reported on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet.

Run on ViT alone, that protocol would not have picked the placement we recommend. On the \GainsSelectionHalfN{} selection-half models its twin, token masking on the attention branch output, reads \GainsSelectionHalfTwin{} against \GainsSelectionHalfOurs{}, a gap of \GainsSelectionHalfTwinGap{}. On the \GainsReportHalfN{} report-half models the order reverses, \GainsReportHalfOurs{} against \GainsReportHalfTwin{}. The 2 are tied over the whole panel, so the ViT protocol cannot separate them and the data it selects on leans to the twin. The recommendation rests on 3 things the ViT selection half does not supply: on Swin-S the same protocol picks the attention input by \SwinSelectionHalfTwinGap{} (\cref{sec:swin}), the attention input is the more stable of the 2 across training seeds, and it is the site whose mechanism \cref{sec:mechanism} can state. The numbers in the main text pool both halves.

\section{The shift ratio target}
\label{app:rate}

The original rule reads each placement at the rate where 80\% of clean validation predictions change. \Cref{fig:shift-ladder} checks that target on our models by interpolating every model's rate ladder onto a shared grid of achieved shift ratios from 0.2 to 0.98 and reading AUROC and TPR along it, for all models and separately for the models at each poison rate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_shift_ladder.pdf}
\caption{Mean AUROC and TPR at the 10\% and 20\% false-positive budgets of PSBD-TM against the achieved clean shift ratio. The dashed line marks the adaptive target of \AdaptiveShiftTarget{}. Above it fewer models reach the target within the swept rate ladder and the mean is taken over those that do.}
\label{fig:shift-ladder}
\end{figure}

AUROC barely moves with the target, at \LadderAurocAtZeroSix{} at a shift ratio of \PlacementMatchTarget{} and \LadderAurocAtZeroEight{} at \AdaptiveShiftTarget{}, while the true-positive rate at a fixed false-positive budget keeps rising with it. At a 10\% budget it goes from \LadderTprOneZeroAtZeroSix{} to \LadderTprOneZeroAtZeroEight{} and \LadderTprOneZeroAtZeroNine{} at a shift ratio of 0.9. A higher target therefore buys operating-point performance without moving the ranking statistic. The caveat is that only \LadderAurocAtZeroNineN{} of the \LadderAurocAtZeroEightN{} models reach 0.9 within the swept rate ladder, so the means past \AdaptiveShiftTarget{} are over a shrinking subset. We keep the original target, which sits at the knee of every curve. These readings interpolate each model's ladder to the exact target, which is why they differ slightly from the adaptive rule's, which takes the smallest swept rate past it.

\section{Comparison with Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al. in detail}
\label{app:picek}

Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al.~\cite{karayalcin2026backdoor} remove a backdoor by projecting the direction of 1 selected layer out of every weight matrix that writes to the residual stream, the patch embedding and every attention and MLP output projection. They report attack success falling from \cited{97.7} to \cited{6.7} percent on average. Blend on CIFAR-100 is their 1 survivor. \Cref{tab:erasure} repeats their procedure on our models with their layer selection rule. It adds 2 controls, the same edit restricted to blocks 10 and 11 and the same edit with a random direction of the same norm.

\IfFileExists{tables/erasure.tex}{\input{tables/erasure}}{}
\FloatBarrier

The removal reproduces. Over \ErasureCheckpoints{} checkpoints attack success falls from \ErasureAsrBeforeMean{} to \ErasureAsrAfterAllWritesMean{} and clean accuracy from \ErasureCaBeforeMean{} to \ErasureCaAfterAllWritesMean{}. Blend on CIFAR-100 stays at \ErasureAsrAfterAllWritesMax{} at both poison rates exactly as they report. Neither control moves any attack: \ErasureAsrAfterBlocksOneZeroOneOneMean{} for the blocks 10 and 11 edit and \ErasureAsrAfterRandomDirectionMean{} for the random direction. Our first attempt had edited only the last 2 blocks and left every attack intact, which the blocks 10 and 11 column now shows to be expected. The direction is re-created by every block, so removing it means removing it everywhere.

Their second claim, that distributed triggers reach the class token earlier than patch triggers, is measured independently in \cref{fig:tac-layers}. It holds on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, where the onset of the backdoor direction comes earlier for Blend, BPP and LF than for BadNets. It reverses on GTSRB, which they did not run.

Their third contribution is a data-free detector that scores the classifier head's rows against the early layers' output projections and flags a model when 1 class stands out by more than 3 standard deviations, and it is reported to flag WaNet and BPP reliably and patch triggers never. We ran the rule with their grid over the number of layers and the floor of the standard deviation on \WeightDetectorBackdoored{} of our backdoored models and \WeightDetectorBenign{} benign ones, and \cref{tab:weight-detector} gives every model. It names the correct target on all \WeightDetectorWanetModels{} WaNet models, with a best $Z$ of \WeightDetectorWanetBestZMin{} to \WeightDetectorWanetBestZMax{}. On BPP it names the target on \WeightDetectorBppNamed{} of \WeightDetectorBppModels{} models, and it also names the target of the \WeightDetectorLfModels{} LF model. It never names the target of a BadNets, Blend or TaCT model. On the benign models \WeightDetectorBenignFlagMin{} to \WeightDetectorBenignFlagMax{} percent of the grid flags, every flag a false alarm. Their WaNet result reproduces and their BPP result reproduces only in part, and \cref{tab:direction-ablation} says why a weight-space rule reads little.
