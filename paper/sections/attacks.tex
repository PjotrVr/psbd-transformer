\section{The attacks}
\label{app:attacks}

BadNets stamps a small fixed patch onto an image and swaps its label to a chosen target, the earliest data-poisoning backdoor proposed for deep networks \cite{gu2017badnets}. Our patch is a 3 by 3 checkerboard, alternating black and white squares starting from white, stamped into the bottom right corner, the convention BackdoorBench uses on 32 by 32 inputs. The default run is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0, and we separately train an all-to-all variant that maps each source class to the next class, wrapping the last class back to class 0, for the multiclass comparisons elsewhere in the paper. BadNets implants on every dataset and rate we test, with attack success no lower than 0.997 even at 1\% poisoning. A control run trained at target class 1 on GTSRB, meant to check whether shifting the target confounds the clean-label results, collapsed instead to a single-class predictor mid-training, a training divergence covered in the protocol paragraph below rather than a property of this trigger.

Blend alpha-blends a fixed pattern over the whole image rather than localizing to a patch \cite{chen2017targeted}. We replace the paper's Hello Kitty image with a pattern of independent uniform pixels drawn once from a seeded generator, so the package ships no external image, and blend it at a ratio of 0.2 against the clean image. Labeling is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0. Blend implants at every rate on all 4 main datasets, with attack success between 0.989 and 1.000, and it is also the attack used to confirm that SVHN and EuroSAT can host a backdoor at all, clearing at their single trained rate of 10\%. Its own GTSRB target-1 control run collapsed in the same way as the BadNets one above.

SIG adds a horizontal sinusoidal signal across the image rather than a spatial patch and keeps every label unchanged, so only images that already carry the target label are perturbed \cite{barni2019sig}. We use Barni et al.'s own amplitude of 0.157 in 0-to-1 pixel units, 40 over 255, at a frequency of 6 cycles across the image width, clamped back into the valid pixel range after adding the signal. The label mode is clean-label with a single target class, class 0 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, class 1 on GTSRB and SVHN where the default class is too small to reach a usable rate, and class 0 again on EuroSAT. SIG only clears the attack success bar on CIFAR-10 at 10\% poisoning, on EuroSAT at 5\% and 10\%, and on SVHN at 5\% and 10\%. It never clears on CIFAR-100, GTSRB or Tiny ImageNet at any rate we could reach given the clean-label ceiling, staying between 0.076 and 0.673 there, which is why the results in the main text treat it as one of the hard attacks.

WaNet moves where pixels are sampled from rather than changing their values, warping the image through a smooth field generated from a small control grid \cite{nguyen2021wanet}. Our control grid is 4 by 4 points, the warp strength is 0.5, and the field is drawn from seed 0 and shared by every poisoned image. Training also uses WaNet's noise mode, cover images warped by an independent random field per sample with their label kept, at twice the poisoning rate, following BackdoorBench's cross-ratio of 2 and the original paper. Labeling is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0. WaNet never clears the attack success bar at 1\% poisoning on any dataset, reaching only 0.057 to 0.178 there. It clears at 5\% and 10\% on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet, only at 10\% on GTSRB, and never on CIFAR-100, where the best reading is 0.793 at 10\%.

LF adds a perturbation whose energy sits in low spatial frequencies, built by low-pass filtering a fixed noise pattern in the Fourier domain and rescaling it to span the full pixel range \cite{zeng2021lf}. Our filter keeps frequencies within a radius of 4 around the spectrum center and adds the resulting pattern at a strength of 0.1, clamped back into 0 to 1. Labeling is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0. LF implants at every rate on all 4 main datasets, with attack success between 0.922 and 0.999, which puts it alongside BadNets, Blend and BPP among the attacks that implant most reliably.

Label-Consistent stamps the BadNets checkerboard into all 4 corners of a target-class image rather than 1, and keeps that image's own label, so the trigger alone has to carry the backdoor \cite{turner2019label}. The patch-only version does not implant, because the network can still read the class off the untouched picture, so we follow Turner et al. and first perturb each base image with an untargeted L-infinity PGD attack against a clean surrogate, 100 steps at a step size of 2.5 times epsilon over 100, so that the image stops supporting its own label. We swept epsilon over 8, 16 and 32 over 255 at each dataset's highest reachable clean-label rate and kept the smallest value that clears both the attack success bar and the clean-accuracy tolerance. Only GTSRB, at target class 1, rate 5\% and epsilon 16 over 255, passes, with attack success 0.937 and clean accuracy 0.989. CIFAR-10 reaches an attack success of 0.970 at epsilon 8 over 255, but its clean accuracy falls by 0.094, past the tolerance. CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet never reach the attack success bar at any epsilon we tried, and GTSRB's own pilot runs at the other 2 epsilon values collapsed during training instead of failing to implant.

BPP reduces the color depth of the image rather than adding a pattern to it, and the reduced depth itself is the trigger \cite{wang2022bpp}. We quantize to 3 bits per channel, 8 levels, and leave the paper's Floyd-Steinberg dithering off, since it is a sequential per-pixel operation and would slow dataset construction on Tiny ImageNet without changing which images implant. Training also uses BPP's negative samples, images quantized to a different depth drawn from 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6 bits with their label kept, at a cover rate equal to the poison rate, matching BackdoorBench's ratio of 0.1 negatives at 0.1 poisoning. Labeling is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0. BPP implants at every rate on all 4 main datasets, with attack success between 0.868 and 0.996.

Adaptive-Blend keeps Blend's whole-image pattern but trains against it being detected, using 2 mechanisms together \cite{qi2023adaptive}. Cover images carry the trigger with their label kept, at a cover rate equal to the poison rate, so the latent space of a poisoned image stays close to a clean one. The pattern itself is split into a 4 by 4 grid of squares, and training reveals only half of them, chosen at random per image, while evaluation stamps the whole pattern, so the network has to generalize to a stronger trigger than it ever saw during training. The blend ratio is the same 0.2 as Blend, and labeling is dirty-label all-to-one at target class 0. This is the 1 attack in our set that never clears the attack success bar on any dataset or rate, reaching at most 0.838 on GTSRB at 10\% poisoning, so no Adaptive-Blend checkpoint enters the detection results at all despite being designed specifically to defeat the kind of detector PSBD is.

TaCT poisons only images from a chosen source class and flips them to the target, leaving every other class untouched, so attack success is measured on the source class alone rather than diluted by classes the attack never claims to affect \cite{tang2021demon}. The trigger is the same 3 by 3 corner checkerboard as BadNets, the source class is class 1, and the target is class 0. Training also carries cover images, the trigger stamped on non-source classes with their label kept, at a fixed cover rate of 0.01 regardless of the poison rate, since TaCT's own convention selects covers by class rather than by a multiple of the poisoning rate. TaCT implants at every rate on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, and at 5\% and 10\% on GTSRB. Its GTSRB run at 1\% read an attack success of 0.000 with clean accuracy collapsed to 0.121, the same training divergence recorded for BadNets and Blend above rather than a sign that the trigger failed to implant.

Every attack is poisoned at 1\%, 5\% and 10\% of the training set, the declared rate ladder, except where a label mode makes a rate unreachable. The poisoned indices are drawn without replacement from the pool of images eligible under the attack's label mode, from a seeded generator, and the draw is capped at the pool size when the request exceeds it. A dirty-label attack's pool is every non-target image, or every image for all-to-all, so 1\%, 5\% and 10\% are all reachable. A clean-label attack's pool is only the target class itself, so its reachable ceiling is the size of that class over the size of the training set, 10\% on CIFAR-10, 1\% on CIFAR-100, 0.5\% on Tiny ImageNet and 0.56\% on GTSRB at class 0 or 5.63\% at class 1, and a requested rate above the ceiling silently draws the same index set as the ceiling itself. A backdoored checkpoint enters the results only when its attack success rate, measured on held-out images eligible under the evaluation-time label rule rather than the training-time one, reaches 0.85, and its clean accuracy stays within 5 points of the benign reference trained with the same recipe on the same dataset. A checkpoint whose clean accuracy has collapsed below half that reference is marked diverged and excluded regardless of what its attack success reads, the rule that catches the GTSRB training collapses named above.
