\section{Robustness and cost}
\label{sec:robustness}

\subsection{An adaptive attacker and the union of probes}
\label{sec:robustness:adaptive}

The PSBD paper tests an attacker who knows the defense and adds a loss term that lowers the prediction shift of poisoned samples, and reports that the defense survives because its rate rule moves to a higher dropout rate. We trained a stronger version of that attacker on ViT. It is a hinge loss that pushes the poisoned samples' PSU up to the clean samples' PSU under the exact probe the defender uses, PSBD-TM, trained jointly with the backdoor on CIFAR-100 for BadNets, Blend, BPP, LF and Adaptive-Blend at 3 poison rates. \AdaptiveVitCells{} of the evasive models keep an ASR of at least \AdaptiveHighAsr{} and lose \AdaptiveVitCaCost{} clean accuracy on average. \Cref{tab:adaptive} summarizes them and \cref{tab:adaptive-attacker} lists them.

\input{tables/adaptive_summary}

Against a single fixed probe the attacker wins. PSBD-TM falls from a mean AUROC of \AdaptiveVitProbedBase{} to \AdaptiveVitProbedEvade{} while the attack keeps its success. The rate rule is already inside these numbers and does not rescue the probe. The claim that PSBD survives an adaptive attacker, obtained with a weaker attacker on ResNet-18, does not appear to transfer to ViT as stated.

What does hold is that the evasion does not transfer between probes. The attacker's loss is computed through 1 site and 1 perturbation, so its gradient only touches the features that probe reads. The 2 probes the attacker never saw still read \AdaptiveVitTransfer{} on average on the evasive models, against \AdaptiveVitProbedEvade{} for the probe it trained against.

The defense we recommend is therefore a union of probes. Let $s_j(x)$ be the PSU of input $x$ under probe $j$, and let $r_j(x)$ be its rank, the share of clean validation inputs whose statistic under probe $j$ is below $s_j(x)$, so that a triggered input, which barely shifts, has a rank near 0. The union score is the most suspicious rank across probes,
\begin{equation}
  u(x) = \min_{j} r_j(x),
\end{equation}
and $x$ is flagged when $u(x)$ falls below the 25th percentile of $u$ over the clean validation set. Taking the minimum means an input escapes only when every probe considers it clean, and setting the threshold on $u$ itself rather than on each probe keeps the false-positive rate at the budget whatever the probes' correlation. We ran a union of 3 probes, PSBD-TM, dropout at the attention input and gain scaling of the MLP LayerNorm output, each at its own rate from the same rule. A 4th probe, Gaussian noise at the MLP input, was left out because its statistic reads below chance on several of these models, and a probe below chance only adds false alarms to a minimum.

The union restores a mean AUROC of \AdaptiveVitUnion{} on the evasive models, with the worst case \cref{tab:adaptive} names. At the 25\% budget it flags 0.97 of the triggered inputs at a measured false-positive rate of 0.26. This is within 0.004 of a union that drops the attacked probe as if the defender knew which one it was, so the defender does not need to know. The cost is 1 sweep per probe, 3 times PSBD-TM alone. The open question is an attacker who trains against the union itself. That attack has to evade 3 probes with 3 different Jacobians at once, and evading 1 already costs up to 4.5 clean-accuracy points, but we have not run it.

The union is not only for the adaptive case. \Cref{tab:probe-union} reads the same rule on the 65 ordinary models, where nobody trained against a probe. Adding PSBD-RD to PSBD-TM rescues the 2 models PSBD-TM misses, WaNet on CIFAR-10 from \ProbeUnionWanetCifarOneZeroTm{} to \ProbeUnionWanetCifarOneZeroTmRd{} and SIG on CIFAR-10 from \HeadlineFloorAuroc{} to a comparable level. It costs on TaCT, where PSBD-RD reads a coin and its rank drags the minimum down, so the paired gain over all models is \ProbeUnionTmRdGain{} with an interval \ProbeUnionTmRdGainCi{} that crosses 0. Pairing PSBD-TM with token masking on the attention branch output instead gains \ProbeUnionTmBranchGain{} with an interval that excludes 0, because the 2 probes fail on different models. The 3-probe union of the adaptive section reads \ProbeUnionThreeProbeAuroc{} on the \ProbeUnionThreeProbeN{} models holding all 3 probes, and a union of every fully swept placement gains less than the 2-probe one. More probes help when they disagree in the right way, not by number.

\subsection{Forward passes and their cost}
\label{sec:robustness:passes}

PSBD uses $k = 3$ passes. Over all \PassAllCells{} models the mean AUROC of PSBD-TM rises from \PassAllAurocKOne{} at $k = 1$ to \PassAllAurocKTwo{} at $k = 2$ and \PassAllAurocKThree{} at $k = 3$. On the \PassPilotCells{} models whose sweep reached 20 passes it rises from \PassPilotAurocKOne{} to \PassPilotAurocKThree{} at 3 passes and \PassPilotAurocKTwoZero{} at 20, with TPR at a 10\% false-positive budget going from \PassPilotTprOneZeroKOne{} to \PassPilotTprOneZeroKThree{} to \PassPilotTprOneZeroKTwoZero{}. The second and third pass are worth having on both populations. Beyond the third the gain is \PassPilotAurocGainBeyondThree{} AUROC. That is small but not negligible, and it is a correction: an earlier reading on a pilot of 8 models put it under 0.003, which the larger population does not support. The remaining models have not been swept past 3 passes, so we carry the original work's $k = 3$ rather than claim it is converged. \Cref{app:passes} plots both curves as the gain over a single pass, which is the comparison 2 populations license.

\IfFileExists{tables/cost.tex}{\input{tables/cost}}{}

The passes are the cost of the defense. Each pass is a full forward pass of the perturbed network, and \cref{tab:cost} gives the measured cost on the A100 we use. At batch size 128 a plain ViT-B/16 prediction takes \CostPlainSecondsVit{} seconds per input. PSBD at $k = 3$ costs \CostSlowdownKThreeVit{} that. At $k = 20$ it costs \CostSlowdownKTwoZeroVit{}. Swin-S is faster in absolute terms at every $k$ with the same ratios, \CostSlowdownKThreeSwin{} at 3 passes. A deployment therefore trades a 3-fold slowdown for the detector at $k = 3$ and would pay a further 7-fold for the \PassPilotAurocGainBeyondThree{} that $k = 20$ adds.

\subsection{Seed variance}
\label{sec:robustness:seeds}

\SeedCells{} backdoored models were trained \SeedsPerCell{} times with different seeds (BadNets, Blend and WaNet on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet). PSBD-TM has a seed standard deviation of \SeedSdRecommended{} AUROC on average. PSBD-RD has \SeedSdPublished{}, reaching \SeedSdGainMax{} on its worst model. The gain of PSBD-TM over PSBD-RD is \SeedGainMean{} on these models and changes sign on \SeedGainSignFlips{} of \SeedCells{}, all on Blend and WaNet where both placements are near the ceiling. A single seed is therefore a fair reading of PSBD-TM and an unreliable reading of PSBD-RD, which is weaker on average and far less stable. \Cref{app:seeds} lists the per-seed readings, and 2 further seeds of every model are being trained.

\subsection{Comparison with other input-level detectors}
\label{sec:robustness:detectors}

We ported \DetectorsComparedCount{} input-level detectors to exactly the splits, the threshold rule and the false-positive budgets PSBD is scored on, each checked numerically against its reference implementation, and ran all of them on all \DetectorsComparedModels{} backdoored models. \Cref{tab:detectors-auroc} is the result. Rows are attacks and columns defenses, so a reader asking whether anything catches a given attack reads across 1 line.

\input{tables/detectors_auroc}

The placement is what makes the method competitive. Dropout after both residual adds, the placement the PSBD paper published, ranks \DetectorsAurocRankPublished{} of \DetectorsAurocDefensesRanked{} defenses by mean AUROC, with \DetectorsAurocBeatingPublished{} detectors above it. Token masking at the attention input ranks \DetectorsAurocRankOurs{}, and it ranks \DetectorsTprOneZeroRankOurs{} at both false-positive budgets as well. PSBD is not a strong defense on a Vision Transformer as published. It becomes competitive when the perturbation moves.

The margin over the field is thin and we state it as such. The strongest competitor is \DetectorsAurocBestCompetitorName{} at \DetectorsAurocBestCompetitor{} mean AUROC against our \DetectorsAurocOurs{}, a gap of \DetectorsAurocMargin{}. At a 10\% false-positive budget the gap is \DetectorsTprOneZeroMargin{}. At 20\% it is \DetectorsTprTwoZeroMargin{} and we rank \DetectorsTprTwoZeroRankOurs{} rather than first. The honest claim is a tie with the best calibrated port and a clear lead over the other defenses, not a new state of the art.

2 ports fail for reasons specific to ViTs, and the failures are informative. SentiNet's Grad-CAM \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} mask covers 0 to 2\% of the trigger at every block, so its transplant carries nothing and its mean AUROC of \DetectorsAurocSentinet{} sits below chance. IBD-PSC at the paper's amplification factor of 1.5 saturates on a ViT that keeps every prediction and reads \DetectorsAurocIbdPsc{}, while the calibrated port that searches the factor per model reads \DetectorsAurocBestCompetitor{}. The difference between those 2 numbers is the whole method, so an uncalibrated port is not evidence that IBD-PSC fails on ViT, and we count only the calibrated one as the competitor. \Cref{app:detectors} gives both false-positive budgets per attack.

A cheaper alternative to PSBD from our own work reads the class token through the final LayerNorm and head at every block, a logit lens \cite{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,belrose2023tunedlens} that costs 1 pass. Over the \DepthCells{} compared models it reads \DepthAurocAll{} AUROC against PSBD's \HeadlineAurocAdaptive{}, a paired difference of \DepthMinusPsbdAuroc{} \DepthMinusPsbdAurocCi{} that contains 0. On the hard attacks it beats PSBD at the smallest budget by \DepthMinusPsbdTprHard{} \DepthMinusPsbdTprHardCi{}, and it wins that budget on \DepthTprWins{} of the \DepthCells{} models. It reads \DepthBenignAuroc{} on the benign references, so its sign convention is right. The 2 statistics are complementary rather than redundant.
