\section{Robustness and cost}
\label{sec:robustness}

\subsection{An adaptive attacker and the union of probes}
\label{sec:robustness:adaptive}

The PSBD paper tests an attacker who knows the defence and adds a loss term that lowers the prediction shift of poisoned samples, and reports that the defence survives because its rate rule moves to a higher dropout rate. We trained a stronger version of that attacker on ViT. It is a hinge loss that pushes the poisoned samples' PSU up to the clean samples' PSU under the exact probe the defender uses, PSBD-TM, trained jointly with the backdoor on CIFAR-100 for BadNets, Blend, BPP, LF and Adaptive-Blend at 3 poison rates. 14 of the 15 evasive models keep an ASR of at least 0.9 and lose 3 points of clean accuracy on average, and \cref{tab:adaptive} summarises them.

Against a single fixed probe the attacker wins. PSBD-TM falls from a mean AUROC of 0.966 to 0.233, and to 0.004 on BadNets at 5\%, while the attack keeps its success. The rate rule is already inside these numbers and does not rescue the probe. The claim that PSBD survives an adaptive attacker, obtained with a weaker attacker on ResNet-18, does not appear to transfer to ViT as stated.

What does hold is that the evasion does not transfer between probes. The attacker's loss is computed through 1 site and 1 perturbation, so its gradient only touches the features that probe reads. Dropout at the attention input still reads 0.81 on average on the evasive models and gain scaling of the MLP LayerNorm output reads 0.98, against 0.23 for the probe the attacker trained against.

The defence we recommend is therefore a union of probes. Let $s_j(x)$ be the PSU of input $x$ under probe $j$, and let $r_j(x)$ be its rank, the share of clean validation inputs whose statistic under probe $j$ is below $s_j(x)$, so that a triggered input, which barely shifts, has a rank near 0. The union score is the most suspicious rank across probes,
\begin{equation}
  u(x) = \min_{j} r_j(x),
\end{equation}
and $x$ is flagged when $u(x)$ falls below the 25th percentile of $u$ over the clean validation set. Taking the minimum means an input escapes only when every probe considers it clean, and setting the threshold on $u$ itself rather than on each probe keeps the false-positive rate at the budget whatever the probes' correlation. We ran a union of 3 probes, PSBD-TM, dropout at the attention input and gain scaling of the MLP LayerNorm output, each at its own rate from the same rule. A 4th probe, Gaussian noise at the MLP input, was left out because its statistic reads below chance on several of these models, and a probe below chance only adds false alarms to a minimum.

The union restores a mean AUROC of 0.967 on the evasive models, with a worst case of 0.852 on LF at 1\%, and at the 25\% budget it flags 0.97 of the triggered inputs at a measured false-positive rate of 0.26. This is within 0.004 of a union that drops the attacked probe as if the defender knew which one it was, so the defender does not need to know. The cost is 1 sweep per probe, 3 times PSBD-TM alone. The open question is an attacker who trains against the union itself. That attack has to evade 3 probes with 3 different Jacobians at once, and evading 1 already costs up to 4.5 clean-accuracy points, but we have not run it. We are also training the PSBD paper's own adaptive attacker, whose loss is a convex combination of the backdoor loss and the absolute prediction shift, and \cref{app:rate} will carry its numbers.

\subsection{Forward passes and their cost}
\label{sec:robustness:passes}

PSBD uses $k = 3$ passes. On all models the mean AUROC of PSBD-TM rises from 0.919 at $k = 1$ to 0.930 at $k = 2$ and 0.935 at $k = 3$. On 6 models at 1\% poisoning we also swept $k$ up to 20, and there AUROC goes from 0.963 at 1 pass to 0.972 at 3 and 0.974 at 20, with TPR at a 10\% false-positive budget from 0.940 to 0.967 to 0.969. The second and third pass are worth having, and the gain from the third to the twentieth is under 0.003. \Cref{app:passes} plots the full curves and the sweeps over more models and more values of $k$ that are running.

\IfFileExists{tables/cost.tex}{\input{tables/cost}}{}

The passes are the cost of the defence. Each pass is a full forward pass of the perturbed network, and \cref{tab:cost} gives the measured cost on the A100 we use. At batch size 128 a plain ViT-B/16 prediction takes 2.2 ms per input, PSBD at $k = 3$ takes 6.7 ms, 3.0 times as much, and $k = 20$ takes 46 ms, 21 times as much. Swin-S is a third faster at every $k$ with the same ratios. A deployment therefore trades a 3-fold slowdown for the detector at $k = 3$ and would pay a further 7-fold for the 0.003 that $k = 20$ adds.

\subsection{Seed variance}
\label{sec:robustness:seeds}

14 backdoored models were trained 3 times with different seeds (BadNets, Blend and WaNet on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet). PSBD-TM has a seed standard deviation of 0.012 AUROC on average. PSBD-RD has 0.076, and reaches 0.278 on CIFAR-100 BadNets at 5\%, where its 3 seeds read 0.826, 0.493 and 0.274. The gain of PSBD-TM over PSBD-RD is +0.128 on these models and changes sign on 4 of 14, all on Blend and WaNet where both placements are near 1.0. A single seed is therefore a fair reading of PSBD-TM and an unreliable reading of PSBD-RD, which is weaker on average and far less stable. \Cref{app:seeds} lists the per-seed readings, and 2 further seeds of every model are being trained.

\subsection{Comparison with other input-level detectors}
\label{sec:robustness:detectors}

We ported 10 input-level detectors to the same splits and threshold rule (confidence, STRIP, SCALE-UP in 2 variants, IBD-PSC, TeCo, CD-L, Beatrix, TED, SentiNet), each checked numerically against its reference implementation. On a smoke test of 3 GTSRB models every detector reads chance on the benign model, and on BadNets STRIP, CD-L, TED and TeCo all reach 0.99 or more. On a patch attack at 5\% poisoning PSBD has no edge over them.

2 ports fail for reasons specific to ViTs. IBD-PSC amplifies LayerNorm gains at the paper's factor of 1.5, and a 99\%-accurate ViT keeps every prediction, so its score saturates at an AUROC of 0.506. SentiNet's Grad-CAM \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} mask covers 0 to 2\% of the trigger at every block, so its transplant carries nothing and reads 0.107. The full comparison over every backdoored model is running, and \cref{app:smoke} gives the smoke test.

A cheaper alternative to PSBD from our own work reads the class token through the final LayerNorm and head at every block, a logit lens \cite{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,belrose2023tunedlens} that costs 1 pass. It ties PSBD on AUROC over all models (0.911 against 0.935) and beats it on TPR at a 1\% false-positive budget on BPP, WaNet, TaCT and SIG, by +0.183 with an interval from +0.062 to +0.296, and it loses on BadNets. The 2 statistics are complementary rather than redundant.
