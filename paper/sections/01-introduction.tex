\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A backdoored classifier behaves normally on clean inputs and predicts an attacker's class whenever a trigger appears in the input. The attacker plants the behaviour by poisoning a small share of the training data, often 1\% or less, and the defender receives the trained model without ever seeing that data. Input-level detection asks the most practical question in this setting. Given the deployed model and 1 incoming input, is that input carrying a trigger? A detector that answers well runs in front of the model in production, needs no retraining, and can be paired with a removal defence later.

Prediction Shift Backdoor Detection (PSBD) \cite{li2025psbd} is such a detector. Its observation is that a backdoored input is decided by a shortcut the model learned quickly and firmly, while a clean input is decided by a broad set of features. When the network is perturbed at inference the broad decision wobbles and the shortcut holds. PSBD therefore runs the model a few times with dropout active, measures how much the probability of the predicted class falls, and flags the inputs whose prediction barely moves. It needs only a small clean validation set to set its threshold, and on ResNet-18 and VGG-16 it reports strong results across 7 attacks.

Vision Transformers (ViTs) \cite{dosovitskiy2021an} now sit in most vision pipelines, and the PSBD paper lists them as future work. Nothing in the prediction shift argument depends on convolutions, but the perturbation itself does not carry over unchanged. A ResNet basic block has 1 natural site, the residual stream after the add, and dropout there is the only reasonable perturbation, so the original work never had to choose.

A ViT block has several sites. There is the input to attention, the input to the MLP, each branch output before its residual add, the residual stream after each add, the attention heads and the MLP hidden units, and each of these sees a different tensor with a different role. Dropout is also not the only way to perturb them. Whole tokens can be masked, channels can be masked, noise can be added before a LayerNorm, or its gain can be scaled, and each interacts differently with the attention and the normalisation that follow. Adapting PSBD to a ViT is therefore a design question with 2 axes, the site and the perturbation, and this paper answers it.

We ran the search over both axes on a fixed set of backdoored models and found a clear winner. Masking whole tokens at the input of every attention block, before its LayerNorm, detects poisoned inputs with a mean AUROC of 0.935 over 65 backdoored ViT-B/16 models spanning 4 datasets, 7 attacks and 3 poison rates, and reaches 0.968 on Swin-S. The most direct transfer of the original idea, dropout on the residual stream after each add, reaches 0.832 on the same models and fails on 12 of them. We show once, in \cref{sec:results:headline}, where that difference comes from.

The rest of the paper asks why attention-input token masking wins, since the reason is what makes the finding transferable. Following the backdoor through the network with activation patching, direction ablation and a per-layer read of the class token gives an account that predicts which sites and perturbations work, why the ranking of perturbations reverses between 2 sites, which attacks show the prediction shift phenomenon the original paper describes, and how the picture carries over to Swin.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A placement of PSBD for ViTs, token masking at the attention input, selected over a fixed basis of 18 placements on 65 backdoored ViT-B/16 models under 1 decision rule, with a mean AUROC of 0.935, a true-positive rate of 0.629 at 1\% false positives, and its largest advantage at 1\% poisoning (\cref{sec:results}).
  \item A separation of site from perturbation, showing that both matter and that neither reduces to a family. The same perturbation moves by 0.109 between the attention and the MLP input, the same site moves by 0.206 between masking and noise, and the perturbation effect reverses sign between the 2 sites (\cref{sec:results:operator}).
  \item A mechanistic account, tested on the same models, in which the backdoor is 1 direction routed through attention from the trigger's tokens to the class token in the last third of the network. The account explains the winning placement, the choice of whole-token masking over dropout, the reversal through LayerNorm absorption, and the attacks on which the original paper's prediction shift phenomenon does and does not appear (\cref{sec:mechanism}).
  \item A transfer of the placement to Swin-S, together with the qualified results a deployer needs. A union over probes defends against an adaptive attacker who trains against a single probe, extra forward passes help at low poison rates, and the all-to-all attack remains the open case (\cref{sec:swin,sec:robustness,sec:limitations}).
\end{enumerate}
Every number in this paper is produced by a script from a coverage ledger that declares the set of models, and the code, the ledger and the generators are released.
