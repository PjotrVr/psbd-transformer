\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and training.}
Every model is a torchvision \cite{paszke2019pytorch} ViT-B/16 or Swin-S with ImageNet weights, fine-tuned for 15 epochs with Adam \cite{kingma2015adam} at a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 128, no dropout and no augmentation beyond the dataset's normalisation. Training a backdoored model takes 45 to 170 minutes on 1 A100 depending on the dataset.

\paragraph{Datasets and attacks.}
The datasets are CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 \cite{krizhevsky2009cifar}, GTSRB \cite{stallkamp2012gtsrb} and Tiny ImageNet \cite{le2015tiny}, with SVHN \cite{netzer2011svhn} and EuroSAT \cite{helber2019eurosat} added for the clean-label attacks that the other datasets cannot carry. The attacks are BadNets \cite{gu2017badnets}, Blend \cite{chen2017targeted}, WaNet \cite{nguyen2021wanet}, BPP \cite{wang2022bpp}, the low-frequency attack LF \cite{zeng2021lf}, TaCT \cite{tang2021demon}, SIG \cite{barni2019sig}, Label-Consistent \cite{turner2019label} and Adaptive-Blend \cite{qi2023adaptive}, each at poison rates of 1\%, 5\% and 10\% of the training set. \Cref{app:attacks} describes every attack and the exact trigger and parameters we used.

\paragraph{Terminology and metrics.}
We use the vocabulary of the backdoor literature. The poisoning rate is the share of the training set the attacker modifies. An attack is dirty-label when it changes the labels of the poisoned images and clean-label when it does not, all-to-one when every trigger maps to 1 target class and all-to-all when each source class maps to its own target, and source-specific when only chosen source classes are poisoned. The attack success rate (ASR) is the share of triggered non-target test images classified as the target, and clean accuracy (CA) is the accuracy of the backdoored model on clean test images, whose drop against the benign model of the same dataset measures the attack's cost. For the detector, a positive is a triggered input. The true-positive rate (TPR, the detector's sensitivity) is the share of triggered inputs flagged, the false-positive rate (FPR, 1 minus its specificity) is the share of clean inputs flagged, and AUROC is the area under the curve of TPR against FPR over every threshold. We write TPR@FPR for the TPR read at a fixed FPR budget, and the budget is set on the clean validation set.

\paragraph{Which models are evaluated.}
A backdoored model enters the detection results only if its ASR is at least 0.85 and its CA is within 5 points of the benign reference on the same dataset. A detector cannot be credited or blamed for a backdoor that was never planted. Of the 105 models we trained, 71 pass this bar, and 65 of them have been swept over every placement of the basis. The results of \cref{sec:results} are read on those 65 models and \cref{app:results} lists every one of them with its ASR, clean accuracy and detection numbers.

\paragraph{Splits and uncertainty.}
A fixed permutation of each test set gives a 2000-image clean validation set for the threshold and the rate rule, and an analysis pool for the paired clean and triggered sets. Nothing from the validation set enters the analysis pool. Every gain between 2 placements is a paired difference on the same model, averaged over models, with a 95\% bootstrap interval \cite{efron1993bootstrap} over models from 5000 resamples. To guard against selecting the best placement on the same models it is reported on, the basis declares a split before any sweep, and \cref{app:protocol} shows that the winning placement holds on the held-out half.
