\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Attacks.}
The attacks we use span patch triggers (BadNets \cite{gu2017badnets}), blended and low-frequency global triggers (Blend \cite{chen2017targeted}, LF \cite{zeng2021lf}), warping (WaNet \cite{nguyen2021wanet}), source-specific triggers (TaCT \cite{tang2021demon}), clean-label poisoning (SIG \cite{barni2019sig}, Label-Consistent \cite{turner2019label}) and an attack designed against input-level detectors (Adaptive-Blend \cite{qi2023adaptive}). \Cref{app:attacks} describes each as we run it.

\paragraph{Input-level detectors.}
The defences closest to ours decide per input at test time from the deployed model and a small clean set. STRIP \cite{gao2019strip} superimposes clean images and reads the entropy of the predictions. SCALE-UP \cite{guo2023scale} amplifies pixel values and reads label consistency. IBD-PSC \cite{hou2024ibdpsc} amplifies normalisation parameters and reads retained confidence. TeCo \cite{liu2023teco} reads the spread of corruption severities at which the prediction flips. CD-L \cite{huang2023distilling} distils a minimal input mask, Beatrix \cite{ma2023beatrix} reads Gram-matrix deviations, TED \cite{mo2024ted} reads the rank trajectory of an input's neighbours across layers, and SentiNet \cite{chou2020sentinet} transplants a salient region onto clean images.

PSBD \cite{li2025psbd} belongs to the consistency family with STRIP, SCALE-UP and TeCo. All 4 perturb the model or its input and read how stable the prediction is, and they differ in what they perturb. To our knowledge no earlier work studies what happens to such a detector when the perturbation site is a design choice, as it is on a ViT. Prediction depth \cite{baldock2021difficulty} reads a network's intermediate layers through its own head, which we use as a 1-pass comparison.

\paragraph{Training-time detection and removal.}
Detectors that need the poisoned training pool (Spectral Signatures \cite{tran2018spectral}, Activation Clustering \cite{chen2018activation}, SCAn \cite{tang2021demon}, SPECTRE \cite{hayase2021spectre}) and defences that retrain or prune the model (fine-pruning \cite{liu2018finepruning}, NAD \cite{li2021nad}, ANP \cite{wu2021anp}, I-BAU \cite{zeng2022ibau}, CLP \cite{zheng2022clp}) answer a different question from ours and need access we do not assume. The SAM-enhanced detection paper \cite{zhang2024samdetection} trains the victim with sharpness-aware minimisation to amplify backdoor neurons and improve training-set sanitisation, a setting we do not evaluate here.

\paragraph{Mechanistic work on ViT backdoors.}
Karayal\c{c}{\i}n et al.~\cite{karayalcin2026backdoor} estimate a trigger direction in ViT activations and remove the backdoor by orthogonalising it out of the weights. \Cref{sec:mechanism:picek} states where we agree and reproduces their removal. Activation patching \cite{zhang2024patching} and the logit lens \cite{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,belrose2023tunedlens} are the tools we use to locate the backdoor by depth, and the register and attention-sink literature \cite{darcet2024registers,sun2024massive} informs our reading of how a patch trigger is routed. Monte Carlo dropout as an uncertainty estimate \cite{gal2016dropout} is the reading of PSBD's statistic our margin account rests on.
