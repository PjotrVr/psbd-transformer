\begin{abstract}
Prediction Shift Backdoor Detection (PSBD) flags a poisoned input by how little its prediction moves when the network is perturbed with dropout at inference. It was designed for ResNets, where dropout on the residual stream is the natural perturbation. We adapt the idea to Vision Transformers (ViTs), where a block offers several sites and several kinds of perturbation, and we show that this choice decides the outcome. Masking whole tokens at the input of every attention block reaches a mean AUROC of 0.935 over backdoored ViT-B/16 models spanning 4 datasets, 7 attacks and poison rates from 1\% to 10\%, flags 80\% of triggered inputs at a 10\% false-positive rate, and reaches 0.968 on Swin-S. We explain the ranking of sites and perturbations by following the backdoor through the network. It is 1 direction in the residual stream, written in the last third of the network and routed from the trigger's tokens to the class token through attention, so removing tokens before attention removes the shortcut, while noise at the same site is absorbed by the LayerNorm behind it. We also report the limits. An attacker who trains against 1 probe defeats it, a union of probes restores detection, and all-to-all attacks remain open.
\end{abstract}
