\section{Swin Transformer}
\label{sec:swin}

Swin-S restricts attention to shifted windows and merges patches between stages, so tokens mix locally within a stage and only reach every other token through merging. If the account of \cref{sec:mechanism} is right, a token mask before the attention LayerNorm should still remove routed evidence, since every route still passes through windowed attention, and the position should still beat the literal port. \Cref{tab:swin} reads the same placements on 53 to 60 backdoored Swin-S cells over CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and GTSRB (no attack implants on Tiny ImageNet on Swin at any rate we tried).

The ranking transfers. The recommended placement reaches 0.968 with 1 inverted cell against 0.871 and 5 inversions for the literal port, a paired gain of +0.091 close to the ViT gain of +0.103. The third block of the table is the control that separates position from operator on Swin as \cref{tab:operators} did on ViT: moving dropout, the paper's operator, from the residual stream to the attention input gains nothing ($-$0.022, interval containing 0), while moving to token masking at that same input gains +0.091. On Swin as on ViT, the operator that removes whole tokens is what the attention input rewards, and windowing does not change that. 2 differences remain. WaNet is detected worse on Swin than on ViT (0.26 to 0.29 against 0.51 to 0.55 with dropout at the matched rate on CIFAR-100), consistent with a warping trigger that windowed attention spreads across windows before any single window can be masked away. And the Swin cells have not been swept over the full 18-placement basis, so the Swin ranking rests on 5 placements rather than 18; the panel is being completed.
