\section{Transfer to Swin-S}
\label{sec:swin}

Swin-S restricts attention to shifted windows of 7 by 7 tokens and merges patches between its 4 stages, so tokens mix locally within a stage and reach every other token only through merging. That is a strong test of the account in \cref{sec:mechanism}. If detection works because a token mask removes evidence before attention can route it, then windowing should not matter, because every route still passes through a windowed attention. If instead the attention input is merely a convenient place to inject noise, windowing should degrade it.

\paragraph{The ranking transfers.}
Token masking at the attention input reads a mean AUROC of \SwinRecommendedAurocAdaptive{} over \SwinRecommendedN{} models, against \SwinPublishedAurocAdaptive{} for dropout after both residual adds. The paired gain is \SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublished{} with a 95\% interval of [\SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublishedLow{}, \SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublishedHigh{}] over the \SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublishedN{} models carrying both, close to the \HeadlineGainAdaptiveAuroc{} we measure on ViT. At a 10\% false-positive budget it flags \SwinRecommendedTprOneZero{} of triggered inputs against \SwinPublishedTprOneZero{}, and at 20\% \SwinRecommendedTprTwoZero{} against \SwinPublishedTprTwoZero{}.

\paragraph{The operator carries it, not the site.}
Swin admits the same control that separates the 2 axes on ViT in \cref{tab:staircase-operators}. Moving dropout from the residual stream to the attention input, holding the operator fixed, gains \SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublished{} with an interval of [\SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublishedLow{}, \SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublishedHigh{}], which contains 0. Changing the operator at that same site to a token mask gains \SwinGainRecommendedMinusSameSiteDropout{} [\SwinGainRecommendedMinusSameSiteDropoutLow{}, \SwinGainRecommendedMinusSameSiteDropoutHigh{}]. The site alone buys nothing on Swin either. What the attention input rewards is a perturbation that removes whole tokens, and windowing does not change that.

\paragraph{The choice is licensed on the selection half.}
On ViT the recommended placement and its twin, token masking on the attention branch output before the add, take the top 2 places of the basis with a mean AUROC identical to 3 decimal places (\cref{tab:basis-ranking}), which is too little for a declared protocol to choose between them (\cref{app:protocol}). On Swin they are not close. The twin reads \SwinSelectionHalfTwinAuroc{} against \SwinSelectionHalfRecommendedAuroc{} on the \SwinSelectionHalfRecommendedN{} selection-half models, a gap of \SwinSelectionHalfTwinGap{}. The gap holds at \SwinReportHalfTwinGap{} on the \SwinReportHalfRecommendedN{} report-half models. Over the whole Swin panel the recommendation leads its twin by \SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwin{} [\SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwinLow{}, \SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwinHigh{}]. A protocol run on Swin selection data picks the placement we recommend.

\paragraph{Where it fails.}
The mean hides the failures, and the failures are the part a defender needs. \SwinRecommendedBelowChance{} of \SwinRecommendedN{} Swin models reads below chance, \SwinRecommendedFloorCell{} at \SwinRecommendedFloor{}. The \SwinHardestAttacks{} attacks hold the 4 lowest readings, which is the same family that is hardest on ViT: both place the trigger so that no single token carries it alone, Adaptive-Blend by spreading a low-opacity pattern over the image and TaCT by restricting the trigger to source classes the defender does not know. A further \SwinShiftTargetUnreached{} models are not reported at the adaptive rule at all, because their clean-validation shift ratio never reached the \AdaptiveShiftTarget{} target inside the swept rate ladder. We report them as out of scope rather than reading them at a rate the rule did not select.
